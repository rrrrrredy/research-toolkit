AI customer service in production: choosing among three product routes and separating growth from results

Information cutoff: September 7, 2026. Audience: enterprise AI product leads.

English translation of the archived Chinese revised report. This translation preserves the historical claims, examples, links, and limitations; it is not a new research run or an update to current product information. Chinese original.

1. Invest in verifiable business processes, rather than a higher “resolution rate”

Over the next quarter, AI customer-service budgets should prioritize two areas: high-volume requests with explicit rules and verifiable business results, such as checking orders, retrieving receipts, and changing subscriptions; and retrieval, reply suggestions, or ticket preparation where a person makes the final decision. Complex disputes, irreversible financial actions, and high-value customer relationships should not yet be handed over entirely to unattended systems. This recommendation follows from the available product capabilities and evidence limits. It is not a ranking derived from a common production test of the three vendors.

Teams already using Intercom and mainly handling standardized support questions can begin by evaluating Fin. Teams using Zendesk that want to retain their ticketing, routing, and support-management setup can start with its native AI agents. Enterprises whose customer data, orders, and authorization processes are already in Salesforce should first assess Agentforce's integration value. Cross-platform deployment is also a real option: Fin does not require a help-desk migration, and Zendesk's Forethought is sold to users of other platforms. Keeping an existing system is therefore a starting point for comparison, not an exclusive advantage of one vendor. The next three sections explain the evidence behind these judgments. Expansion should depend on whether the customer's problem is actually solved, whether the same problem triggers another contact, whether people perform less work, and whether costs fall after software, maintenance, and checking are included. A vendor may define its own billing rules; a buyer cannot infer cost savings directly from the name of a billable event.

2. Intercom / Fin: keep the help desk, but account for knowledge maintenance and handoffs

Fin's value extends beyond answering help-center questions. Procedures combine natural-language steps, conditional branches, code, and external interfaces. A refund request can trigger order retrieval, an eligibility check, an authorized action, or an escalation to a person. Fin can also work with other help desks. A business with stable support processes can test automation without replacing its ticketing system. But a connectable interface does not mean that the business already has consistent refund policies, correct customer identities, or safe execution permissions. Product capabilities.

Deployment difficulty therefore depends on the rules behind the request, not simply the number of articles in the knowledge base. An order-status query usually has a clear source of truth. Refunds may involve promotional exceptions, partial fulfillment, or fraud risks. The former can often be answered after a lookup; the latter requires an eligibility decision and a transaction result. Track automated lookup, automated execution, and human approval separately. These are implementation judgments based on the product's mechanisms, not measured reliability findings for every Fin use case.

The public price is $0.99 per outcome. Customer-confirmed resolution, an answer followed by no further request, or completion of a Procedure, including a handoff, can qualify. Each conversation is billed only once. Using Fin with Intercom also requires seats: annual-billing starting prices are $29, $85, and $132 per seat per month for Essential, Advanced, and Expert. A standalone connection to an existing help desk does not require Intercom seats, but a minimum monthly commitment applies; the site's example is 50 outcomes. Channel usage can incur additional fees. Intercom pricing and billing definitions.

A handoff can still be valuable. If Fin completes authentication, collects information, and checks eligibility, a person may only need to approve the result. The buyer must distinguish “fewer processing steps” from “no human handling at all.” At the quoted public rate, a purely illustrative billing calculation with 6,000 conversations requiring no person and 1,000 qualifying Procedure handoffs produces an outcome charge of $6,930. Budgeting for only the 6,000 unattended resolutions would omit $990. Actual savings depend on the human work remaining in those 1,000 handoffs. This illustration excludes seats, channel charges, and maintenance; it is not an observed customer invoice.

The Synthesia case is more informative than a resolution-rate claim on its own. In an interview published by Fin, the customer reported a 55% Fin resolution rate and a reduction in total resolution time from five days and five hours to four hours and 37 minutes. It also described contradictory knowledge-base material, extensive reorganization, and dedicated maintenance staff. The case concerns deployment during growth in 2024, not a common test conducted in 2026. A 96% reduction in elapsed resolution time cannot be converted into a 96% reduction in labor costs. Nor can all contributions from the help center, other automation, and knowledge cleanup be assigned to Fin. Synthesia interview.

For buyers, the case supports a conditional choice: test Fin on real tickets when support volume is growing quickly, questions are concentrated, and someone owns the knowledge. When nobody is responsible for conflicting policies, increasing model calls may simply spread conflicting answers faster. Before expanding, establish whether the team can absorb more business without lower quality and whether knowledge maintenance and human fallback remain manageable.

3. Zendesk: connect tickets and automation, but distinguish the dashboard from the bill

Zendesk's procurement rationale begins with the existing support organization. Knowledge, tickets, omnichannel routing, and action-building already share a system; AI can work around those objects. Public annual-billing prices are $55 per agent seat per month for Suite Team and $115 for Suite Professional. These prices cover different seat tiers and are not directly comparable to Fin's price per outcome. Access to AI agents, usage allowances, and add-on products need separate accounting. Zendesk plans.

By the cutoff, Zendesk's AI extended beyond its native help desk. Forethought, available for purchase from June 4, 2026, is an additional product providing intent detection, answers, routing, reply assistance, and quality management, including for other support platforms. This broadens deployment options but adds procurement complexity: expansion of the product range does not give existing plans every new capability for free. Native AI agents and Forethought require distinct confirmation of price and permitted usage. Forethought availability.

A more consequential change for operating analysis is the 2026 definition of automated resolution rate. An update starting on May 18, 2026 expanded the dashboard numerator from Verified to Contained plus Verified, while billing remained tied to Verified. The company would also remap historical conversations to the new definition. An increase between an old dashboard screenshot and the new display cannot be attributed directly to model improvement. Even if the system recalculates historical series, exported comparisons across versions still need the same denominator, channels, and status definitions. Metric-change notice.

Contained means that after an AI reply the customer did not ask another question, provide feedback, or request a person, but the interaction did not pass the full satisfactory-resolution check. Verified passes Zendesk's LLM-based evaluation of the conversation text. That goes beyond simply observing no handoff, but it is still not the business's independent confirmation that an order was corrected, a refund arrived, or a customer will not contact support again. A satisfied-sounding conversation can coexist with a failed backend action. Account and transaction requests therefore require linking conversational outcomes to business-system results. Resolution tiers and channel end times.

This also affects capacity purchases. Zendesk is migrating to resolution allowances covering the applicable tiers. Invoice prices in its help documentation are explicitly placeholders; actual pricing requires a quote, and unused allowances do not automatically roll over. The migration notice also says unified AI agents are now a standard plan component, so the old AI agent – Advanced add-on is no longer needed for those AI-agent features. That does not remove the separate Forethought purchase boundary described above. A seasonal business buying long-term allowances for peak demand may leave capacity unused during quieter periods. If it disables overage purchases, it also needs to know which capabilities stop when the allowance is exhausted. Allowance migration, unified features, and overage rules.

SeatGeek's customer story offers a specific deployment sequence: begin with “Where are my tickets?”, use authenticated, signed-in customers, and retrieve order information through live interfaces. The useful lesson from this vendor-published historical case is to limit the task, connect authentication and order data, and then expand. It does not establish the effectiveness of new billing tiers or the average benefit for ordinary customers. Historical numbers and their evidence limitations appear in the source notes, not in a ranking of the three vendors. SeatGeek historical case.

An existing Zendesk team should therefore start with a small, complete business loop. If the resolution rate rises while repeat contacts, complaints, or manual data entry do not fall, the next investment should improve state updates, knowledge, or handoffs. The dashboard alone is not sufficient grounds for expanding.

4. Salesforce: the more a business action depends on the existing CRM, the more integration value matters

Salesforce's distinction is that support can be an entry point for reading customer and order information, checking permissions, changing business records, and handing work to employees. Its official order-query pricing example includes both authentication and order retrieval. This route merits assessment when the business already maintains these objects and processes in Salesforce. A small team answering public FAQs may not find the extra integration worthwhile. The dividing line is reusable business assets, not a company-size label. Agentforce actions and pricing examples.

Choose the organization-level billing model before costing requests

Public Conversations pricing is $2 per conversation. Flex Credits cost $500 per 100,000 credits, and a standard Agentforce action uses 20 credits, or $0.10. An illustration with two standard actions totals $0.20 in action charges, not the complete service cost. More significantly, one Salesforce organization cannot use Flex Credits and Conversations simultaneously. Moving from Conversations to Flex Credits requires replacing all existing Agentforce Conversation SKUs. This is an organization-level purchasing decision, not a choice of the cheapest unit for each incoming ticket. Pricing, action examples, and mixing restrictions.

Help Agent charges 400 Flex Credits per successful resolution, corresponding to a public price of $2, with unlimited Agentforce actions and Data 360 queries within that resolution. It is not another independent Conversations rate that can be combined freely ticket by ticket. It also does not mean the base license or every external service is free. Help Agent pricing, billing rules.

Qualifying outcomes have clear boundaries. Abandoned conversations, an explicitly negative final response, or escalation to a person or support ticket before feedback do not qualify. Time limits vary by channel: normally two hours for chat, ten minutes for voice, and three days for email. The official example bills a successfully resolved 12-minute voice interaction as two resolutions. A single customer problem can therefore span multiple billing units. The $2 figure should not be read as unlimited follow-up until final resolution. Outcome conditions and duration rules.

Procurement should model the organization's entire request mix: actions in simple queries, retries and updates in complex requests, cross-channel duration, and contract-migration costs. Unit-price comparisons are meaningful only when the candidate plans can actually serve the same need and the license scope applies.

Adoption cases do not establish that effects or integration have already materialized

The Engine story is more specific than a broad “the agent is live” claim. In a June 2026 case, Salesforce said the customer used Agentforce to retrieve bookings, verify authorization, and make changes. Agentforce resolved 50% of chat requests, while support handling time fell by 15%. That describes entry into order operations, beyond knowledge questions. But the 50% denominator is chat requests, not all business across channels, and the 15% figure is not a controlled estimate of headcount reduction. Without human checking and exception-handling costs, the case cannot establish returns for other enterprises. Engine case.

Commercial scale also requires careful interpretation. Salesforce's August 26, 2026 earnings release reported Agentforce ARR above $1.5 billion, growing more than 240% year over year. The quarter's definition included AI products such as Slackbot and Headless 360. ARR is an annualized recurring-revenue measure, not revenue recognized in that quarter, AI customer-service revenue alone, or customer return on investment. It supports a judgment of substantial commercial sales, not a claim of superior customer-service results among the three vendors. FY27 second-quarter results.

The proposed Fin transaction must also be separated from product promises. The June 2026 agreement was approximately $3.6 billion; the August earnings release still expected closing in the following weeks. Based on the latest disclosed progress, Fin and Salesforce could not yet be treated as having unified permissions, contracts, roadmaps, and billing. Long-term contracts should specify support for existing interfaces, renewal terms, and data-migration responsibilities. Assuming integration benefits from acquisition ambitions would count an unfulfilled plan as current procurement value. Acquisition agreement, updated closing expectation.

5. Use real costs and customer outcomes to decide next-quarter expansion

Together, the three sets of cases support a limited but useful conclusion: where identity, knowledge, business data, and handoffs connect, AI has moved beyond demonstration. However, vendors largely select and publish the cases, so they do not establish the average customer's benefit. They also lack common task difficulty, channel mixes, and quality standards needed for an effectiveness ranking.

Independent research offers a more qualified lesson. Generative AI at Work includes 5,172 customer-service workers at one company, of whom 1,636 have observations after AI adoption. The entire sample should not be described as having received the intervention. The study found an average 15% improvement in issues resolved per hour, with uneven gains: less-experienced and lower-skilled workers benefited more; the most experienced and skilled workers saw smaller speed gains and a slight reduction in quality. The system suggested responses while people remained responsible for conversations. It did not validate the three vendors' autonomous agents. Human assistance is consequently a worthwhile investment route, but outcomes should be examined by experience and task difficulty. An average does not justify prescribing the same use for every worker. Study text and sample table.

Allocate budgets by request type. Order queries, receipt retrieval, and subscription actions with explicit eligibility rules are suitable for limited-permission automation, with business-state verification. For complex complaints, policy exceptions, and high-value customers, let AI assemble information and suggest replies while people decide compensation and commitments. When knowledge conflicts are common or business interfaces do not reliably return results, fix the data and handoffs first. Otherwise, more coverage merely shifts human work from answering customers to correcting errors.

Compare economics within a consistent business boundary. For a selected request category:

Cost per genuinely resolved problem = all service costs for that category ÷ problems verified against business outcomes with no repeat contact in the agreed observation window.

The numerator includes AI charges, appropriately allocated software and integration spending, knowledge maintenance, human takeover, sample checks, and error handling. The denominator cannot simply be the platform's “closed tickets,” because customers can contact support through another channel. Also disclose the unresolved share and response time, so avoiding difficult customers cannot make unit costs appear artificially favorable. For assistance-only tasks, compare actual human time and quality on requests of similar difficulty rather than insisting on unattended resolution.

For a small comparison, retain a group of similar requests handled through the existing service, and compare by channel, difficulty, customer type, and time period. That helps separate AI's contribution from seasonal demand, a changing share of easy questions, or concurrent knowledge-base cleanup. A before-and-after comparison can still inform operations when that is all that is available, but it should not establish causal returns.

Review four things weekly over the next quarter. These are management measures a business can implement, not universal industry pass marks:

  1. Was the problem actually solved? Sample requests marked resolved and compare them with order, refund, or permission-change results. Check repeat contacts within an agreed window that fits the business cycle and stays constant during the comparison. If resolution and repeat-contact rates rise together, pause expansion and locate the failing task types.
  2. Did human work actually decline? Separately count automatic completion, AI preparation followed by handoff, and human handling from the start. Track handling time for the latter two. If handoffs or takeover time increase, improve the transferred context first. Steps completed before a handoff do not mean the whole human workload has disappeared.
  3. Are quality costs acceptable? Separate unauthorized financial or account actions, incorrect commitments, and privacy exposure from ordinary wording issues. Stop the affected action and investigate serious overreach; an average satisfaction score cannot offset it. Address ordinary errors by impact and frequency rather than rejecting all automation over a minor wording problem.
  4. Do costs and maintenance remain manageable as coverage grows? Reconcile billable outcomes, Verified resolutions, conversations, or actions with genuinely resolved requests. Include unused prepaid capacity and maintenance hours. Expand only when quality meets requirements, the unresolved share does not worsen, and total costs fall or added capacity has a clear value.

Watch vendor changes through their implications for contracts and operations: whether the Fin–Salesforce transaction closes, whether interfaces and support policies change, whether Zendesk's migration alters usable allowances and prices, and whether Help Agent's included scope applies to the enterprise's plan. Such changes can affect a year's total cost and deserve more procurement attention than a new feature name.
