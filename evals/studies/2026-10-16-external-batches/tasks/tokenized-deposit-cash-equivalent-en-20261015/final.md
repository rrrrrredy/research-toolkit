# Evidence corporate treasuries should require before treating tokenized deposit pilots as operational cash equivalents

Audience: corporate treasury and external auditor liaison. Using the synthetic Bank-T pilot brief, auditor liaison questions, ninety-day control incident log, and peer FAQ, **the judgment is that treasuries should not treat the pilot token as an operational cash equivalent for working-capital reporting until legal classification, stress redeemability, daily GL reconciliation, wallet gating, and reporting labels are evidenced—and even then only with explicit disclosure if covenant metrics change.** Marketing that calls the instrument “on-chain cash equivalent” overreaches the pilot facts. Synthetic materials; no independent review; distinct from private-credit LP on-chain settlement reporting.

## What the pilot actually is

The permissioned token represents a demand-deposit claim at Bank-T, redeemable one-to-one to fiat in banking hours. It is not a free-floating stablecoin; the legal claim remains inside the bank’s insolvency hierarchy. Mint and burn run through the bank API; the chain is largely a messaging and transfer layer among permitted parties. Weekend chain movement does not move fiat until the next business window. That single operational fact creates an intraday working-capital illusion if dashboards label token balances as unrestricted cash.

## Evidence bar versus marketing

Auditor liaison questions set a minimum bar before cash-equivalent treatment: a legal opinion that the instrument meets the applicable cash-equivalent definition; redeemability evidence under stress, not only happy path; dual control on mint/burn keys with daily bank-to-GL reconciliation; segregation so tokens cannot move to unrestricted wallets without a bank gate; and disclosure discipline if the program remains pilot-only—especially where working-capital metrics feed covenants. The bank one-pager’s “on-chain cash equivalent for WC reporting” language fails this bar on its face. Peer FAQ counter-claims—“atomic settlement removes timing risk,” “bankruptcy remote,” “auditor already OK’d”—are unsupported: the fiat leg is banking-hours, the claim sits in the bank hierarchy, and only a liaison Q&A occurred, not a clean classification opinion.

## Control failures already visible in ninety days

Incident one: chain showed credit while the bank core lagged seven hours; a working-capital report nearly filed with inflated cash until reconciliation caught it. Incident two: vendor UI labeled the balance “Cash (on-chain)” until compliance forced “Deposit claim token (pilot).” Incident three: weekend subsidiary transfers created reporting asymmetry versus banking-day cash. Ops conclusion in the log: treat as a restricted digital deposit-claim pilot until redeemability, reconciliation, and legal memo clear—not as operational cash equivalent for covenant working capital.

These incidents are not cosmetic. They show exactly how marketing labels migrate into reporting pipelines. Treasury’s control objective is to stop that migration before auditors or lenders do.

## Counter-claims and how to answer them

Atomic settlement rhetoric collapses when fiat settlement is windowed. Bankruptcy-remote rhetoric collapses when the instrument is still a bank deposit claim. “Other treasuries already book this as cash” is not evidence in the pack and should be ignored until primary documents appear. The productive counter-evidence favoring continued piloting is narrower: faster internal transfer messaging among subsidiaries and richer timestamps for ops—useful, not classification-changing.

## Disclosure stance and change conditions

Near-term disclosure stance: report token balances as restricted pilot deposit claims (or similar), bridge to fiat cash separately, and never blend pending fiat windows into unrestricted cash used for covenants without lender consent. Kill or pause cash-equivalent proposals if daily recon fails, UI labels regress, or legal memo is withheld.

Judgment can change when an independent legal memo affirms classification, two consecutive quarters show clean daily reconciliations under stress drills, UI and reporting labels stay aligned, and covenant lenders consent if metrics change. Until those conditions hold, stronger cash-equivalent language is unsupported.

## Practical checklist for treasury (narrative form)

Before any policy memo claims cash equivalence, treasury should be able to point to: the legal memo file and its date; stress redeemability test results including weekend and bank-holiday scenarios; mint/burn key custody and dual-control evidence; a month of daily recon packs with exception ageing; wallet allowlist controls; and screenshots of reporting labels after compliance review. Missing any one item keeps the instrument out of operational cash equivalent buckets.

## Limitations

Synthetic fixtures; no live web; no independent review; not accounting advice. Replace with counsel- and auditor-specific requirements for the reporting entity’s GAAP/IFRS basis. Keep registries and checklist mechanics out of the reader memo. The durable discipline is boring: labels, recon, redeemability, and legal classification before metaphor.


## How this differs from fund on-chain settlement memos

Private-credit LP reporting questions ask whether a GP’s permissioned settlement pilot changes NAV authority or LPA rights. Corporate treasury questions ask whether a bank-issued deposit token may enter working-capital cash equivalent lines. Shared vocabulary—“on-chain,” “pilot,” “reconciliation”—should not merge the evidence standards. Here the audience needs auditor-ready classification and covenant hygiene; there the audience needs LP disclosure bridges to admin NAV. Using fund-pilot comfort letters to justify treasury cash treatment would be a category error.

## Board-ready wording

“We are piloting a permissioned tokenized deposit claim with Bank-T. Fiat redeemability follows banking hours. We do not currently classify pilot token balances as unrestricted cash equivalents for working-capital or covenant reporting. Balances are labelled as deposit-claim tokens, reconciled daily to bank records, and bridged separately to cash. We will revisit classification only after legal opinion, stress redeemability, sustained clean reconciliations, and any required lender consent.” That paragraph matches the incident log and liaison bar without adopting the marketing one-pager’s leap.
