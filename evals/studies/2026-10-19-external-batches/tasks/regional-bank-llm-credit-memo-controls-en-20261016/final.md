# Controls before regional-bank RMs use third-party LLM credit-memo copilots

Audience: regional-bank credit risk, model risk, and front-line lending ops. Using only the synthetic CreditScribe one-pager, Northlake model-risk gates, shadow-mode QA log, and compliance briefing, **the judgment is that relationship managers should not use a third-party LLM to draft credit-memo substance in production until draft-only attestation, logging/no-default-training, hard blocks on ratings/adverse-action language, and a shadow-mode factual-invent threshold are met; grammar-only assist with numeric hard-blocks may proceed earlier.** Materials are synthetic; no independent review.

## 1. Vendor pitch vs credit-file reality

CreditScribe promises ~45% time savings, “bank-grade security,” and “hallucination controls” via retrieval, with human Accept on risk-rating suggestions. Gaps: no model-risk inventory or adverse-action controls; PDF retention “configurable” without a safe default; a demo snippet invents a DSCR not in the PDF. **Speed claims do not substitute for credit-file integrity.**

## 2. Control map (minimum)

From Northlake’s draft gates, map controls to failure modes:
- **Authority:** outputs are non-authoritative drafts; RM attestation that figures match source documents.
- **Hard blocks:** no auto-accept of risk rating, covenant text, or adverse-action language.
- **Data:** prompt/response logging retained with the credit file; vendor training off by default.
- **Assurance:** ≥60-day shadow mode with dual review; halt if material factual invent rate exceeds threshold.
- **Kill:** silent insertion of ratios/facts not in file; use in declined-file adverse-action letters without counsel.

Compliance briefing adds examiner-facing inventory of tool, data flows, accountability, and sampling — and warns against practice drifting into “AI decisioning” while policy says draft-only.

## 3. Counter-evidence from shadow mode

In 120 sections (weeks 1–8): 9 numeric facts not in source, 14 softened risk language vs RM notes, 6 stale covenants from prior deals, 91 harmless rephrases. Two high-severity invents nearly shipped. Vendor “rare” framing conflicts with QA’s materiality for credit decisions. Pilot RM enthusiasm and under-reported edits mean **surveys cannot replace instrumentation.**

## 4. Phased adoption and kill criteria

**Phase 0:** grammar/structure only; hard-block numbers, ratings, covenants, adverse-action.
**Phase 1:** shadow narrative drafting with dual review and invent-rate dashboard.
**Phase 2:** production draft assist only if Phase 1 thresholds clear and attestation/logging live.

Kill / pause: invent rate above threshold; any silent numeric insertion reaching an approver; retention/training default flipped on without approval; adverse-action use without counsel; examiner disclosure claiming draft-only while RMs treat text as checked. Change to broader use only if invent rate stays below threshold across segments, edit instrumentation shows real human checking, and adverse-action paths remain human-authored.

## 5. Accountability and disclosure stance

Human accountability stays with the RM and approver; the copilot is not a decision system. Disclose to examiners the inventory and sampling results honestly. Do not market “hallucination controls” internally as eliminating invent risk — the shadow log shows residual high-severity errors.

## Limitations

Synthetic pack; one regional bank shadow sample; compliance note not legal advice; no independent review. Live policy needs the bank’s model-risk program and counsel review.

Evidence discipline: figures in the synthetic pack support mechanism judgments only; they are not national statistics. Change the call only after replacing them with auditable ledgers and contract samples.

For decision-makers, a passing long-form memo needs clear mechanisms, counter-evidence on the page, observable change conditions, and explicit limitations. A Lite checklist pass does not certify semantic quality; this task used author self-check without independent review.

If richer samples arrive later, update factor tiers, control maps, and disclosure stubs first, then decide whether to revise the judgment. Optimistic vendor pilots alone should not flip the call.

The one-line conclusion is for decision support, not false certainty. Do not promote vendor packs as audit-ready, or copilots as decision systems, without the gates named above.

Operationalize the kill criteria as an internal checklist: missing any required control keeps the current judgment in force despite commercial pressure.
