# Evidence bar for third-party Scope-3 factors in customer RFQs (through 2027)

Audience: manufacturing sustainability lead and commercial RFQ owners. Using only the synthetic GreenFactor Pro brochure, MidForge internal memo, QA failure extract, and advisor note, **the judgment is that mid-market manufacturers should not treat third-party Scope-3 factor libraries as default RFQ inputs without a tiered evidence bar; spend-based or average-process factors may support screening answers with explicit estimate/non-assurance labels, but not “audit-aligned” or precision claims that buyers could recycle into assured sustainability statements.** Materials are synthetic; no independent review.

## 1. What the vendor pack actually sells

GreenFactor Pro markets 12,000+ factors as “RFQ-ready” and “audit-aligned,” with annual updates from industry averages and public LCA snippets. Fine print states factors are spend-based or average-process, not supplier-specific activity data; default export lacks uncertainty bands; a premium tier adds ± ranges for only 30% of SKUs; the legal footer says the pack is not a substitute for GHG Protocol inventory or third-party assurance. The customer quote (“40 RFQs, zero buyer challenge”) omits n quality and buyer identity. **Treating the brochure badges as an evidence bar would reverse the fine print.**

## 2. Failure modes already inside the plant

QA reviewed 22 questionnaires (2025–2026H1): implied assurance language without a verifier (7), stale factors >3 years (5), single-point numbers with no uncertainty (6), and wrong process-family factors (4). MidForge’s lost-bid anecdote—precise factors that could not be reproduced when a buyer asked for the calculation pack—shows commercial damage from over-precision, even as a single case. The internal memo’s draft bar (supplier-specific for top spend emissions categories; third-party factors only with methodology, vintage ≤3 years, uncertainty, and non-assurance statement; kill on implied ISO/assurance) matches the QA failure distribution.

## 3. Tiered bar through 2027 (recommended)

**Tier A — screening / low-criticality RFQs:** third-party factors allowed if methodology, vintage ≤3 years, and “estimate / not assured inventory” language are visible in the answer pack; no “verified” wording.

**Tier B — high-weighted sustainability scoring or customer public claims:** prefer supplier-specific activity data for the top emissions-driving spend categories; third-party factors only as interim with uncertainty bands and a dated replacement plan.

**Tier C — kill / do not send:** any answer implying assurance without an engagement letter; unreproducible calculation packs; category-mismatched factors; stale factors without disclosure.

The advisor note supports tiering rather than a binary ban: some buyers still accept labeled screening factors, but portals increasingly ask for calculation packs through 2027. Commercial urgency does not justify Tier C language.

## 4. Counter-evidence and change conditions

Counter-evidence to a hard ban: buyers sometimes accept screening factors with clear labels; vendor packs can be faster than primary data collection for long-tail SKUs. Counter-evidence to “RFQ-ready = audit-ready”: brochure footer, QA failure modes, and MidForge reproducibility incident. Change the judgment only if: (1) factors include documented uncertainty and supplier-specific pathways for material categories; (2) RFQ packs are reproducibly archived; (3) assurance language is contractually gated; (4) vintage and category checks are enforced in the send checklist. A clean vendor slide with “audit-aligned” badges does not meet those conditions.

## 5. Disclosure stance for RFQ owners

Default footer for Tier A answers: figures are screening estimates from a named third-party library, vintage dated, not GHG inventory, not third-party assured. Sustainability lead co-signs Tier B/C gates. Sales may not strip uncertainty to “look cleaner.” Through 2027, expect more calculation-pack requests—budget time for reproducibility, not just factor lookup.

## Limitations

Synthetic sources; single mid-market anecdote; QA n=22 internal; advisor note not legal advice; no independent review. Production policy needs the firm’s own GHG boundary and customer contract language.
