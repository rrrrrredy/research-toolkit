# Research brief

## question

Write a complete English-language research report on where growth in China's microdrama market comes from, how distribution and monetization models compete, and how value and risk are distributed across the industry. Use only the case-designated frozen evidence in long_research_microdrama_market, shared with microdrama_market_zh. Do not expand the factual evidence through live research or treat the Chinese report itself as a new source. The evidence cutoff is 2026-11-09. Focus on changes from 2023 through that date, using earlier context when supported by the shared materials. Distinguish year-to-date observations, full-year forecasts, and completed annual results. Keep mainland China's domestic market as the principal scope; overseas operations matter only where they explain domestic participants' resource allocation or strategic choices, and must not be added to domestic market totals.

Define which formats and activities count as microdrama and establish consistent market-size measures. Decompose growth into users, viewing time, consumption frequency, payment conversion, and advertising monetization. Compare user-paid, advertising-supported free, and hybrid models across short-video platforms, mini-programs, and standalone applications. Distinguish user top-ups or spending, advertiser expenditure, platform revenue, production and distribution revenue, transaction value, and net revenue. Address overlapping audiences, multi-channel distribution, and repeated counting of the same money as it passes through the value chain. Explain conflicting market estimates through their definitions, samples, and periods rather than averaging incompatible figures.

Select representative competitors by distribution access, user relationships, content supply, and monetization mechanisms, without imposing a fixed company roster. Compare recommendation and search entry points, paid acquisition, organic discovery, exclusive and non-exclusive content, intellectual-property operations, revenue-sharing rules, and production–distribution relationships. Separate platform effects from differences in content, audience, or development stage. Downloads, registrations, active users, viewers, and paying users are different measures. Use traceable examples of success, adjustment, or exit to examine hit concentration, changing supply, migration toward free viewing, acquisition costs, and retention, without assuming that either free or paid models must prevail.

Follow user and advertiser spending through platforms, media-buying channels, distributors, rights holders, and producers. Identify who recognizes revenue, bears costs, and waits for cash recovery. Provide an auditable value-distribution example using disclosed inputs or explicitly labeled scenarios. Explain how production budgets, acquisition costs, sharing terms, completion rates, and retention affect different participants. Where evidence does not support an industry-wide margin, retain bounded case evidence or ranges rather than extrapolating from exceptional hits. Include content regulation, copyright, protections for minors, refunds, and platform policies where they materially alter operating decisions.

Deliver a coherent report containing a consistently defined market structure table, platform comparison, value-flow analysis, and sensitivity analysis, followed by decision implications for platforms, producers or distributors, and investors. Explain China-specific commercial and regulatory concepts naturally for English-speaking readers without changing the underlying scope. Tables and diagrams may sit within the narrative or an appendix; no fixed headings are required. A summary or platform catalogue cannot replace the causal explanation and comparative cases. Cite the shared materials at the claims they support.

The main checks are whether growth claims have clear audience and revenue denominators, platform differences are explained rather than listed, and acquisition spending, user payments, and downstream revenues are not added together as separate market value. Address contrary evidence and identify conditions that would change the recommendations. The English report may reorganize or reinterpret the shared evidence and need not reproduce the Chinese report's conclusions. Where frozen materials leave a gap, explain its effect instead of filling it with unsupported facts. Growth, fragmentation, slowdown, and unresolved findings are all permissible conclusions.

## audience

English-speaking investors, platform operators, and production or distribution leaders evaluating China's domestic microdrama market

Complete long-form research report final.md with the task-specific comparisons, explanations, tables and locatable citations. Preserve the first draft and actual independent results.

## depth

A full market research report connecting consistent market definitions, growth mechanisms, platform differences, and value-chain economics to decisions for different participants.
