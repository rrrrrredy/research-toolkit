# Token Economics: From Model Costs and Pricing to Workload Value

Author: Codex research producer `/root/ttc_research`. Evidence cutoff: 2026-11-09. This is a revised author outline for independent requirements and structure review, not a completed report. The original Astra planning output and handoff remain unchanged. The case JSON is authoritative.

## Central argument and scope

The economic unit that buyers can optimize is an accepted task delivered within its quality, latency and risk requirements. Token prices affect that unit through a finite workflow, alongside review, tools, failed work and capacity. They do not identify model production costs or private provider margins. The report will explain how an apparently expensive model or a deliberately shorter workflow can be cheaper per accepted task, and where a cheaper model is sufficient.

The representative historical table will cover dated 2024 and 2025 offers and a separately dated 2026 cutoff-period snapshot across OpenAI, Anthropic, Google and DeepSeek. It will prioritize contracts used in the analysis; it will not promise every vendor in every year. Unverifiable dates or geographic scopes will be left explicit. A rate that starts after the cutoff cannot enter the calculations. No API/model experiment is authorized; measured task success rates will not be invented. Public evidence, exact arithmetic on explicit scenario inputs, and judgments will be distinguished.

## Report structure

1. **Buying outcomes under constraints.** A decision-oriented introduction and a cost/price/value map. Define request, attempt, trajectory, incoming task, accepted task and unresolved task. Explain the denominator before comparing tariffs.
2. **Five different ledgers.** Training/development spending, possible investment allocation, inference infrastructure, provider selling prices, and application operation. Reconstruct DeepSeek-V3's reported GPU-hour estimate, contrast a second disclosed training case or documented cost-estimation method, and show transparent recovery scenarios. Explain sunk investment, marginal cost, capacity cost, depreciation and fully loaded cost without claiming a private allocation policy.
3. **Serving mechanisms that move cost.** Active versus resident parameters, prefill and decode, context/KV growth, quantization and hardware kernels, batching, reliability reserves, utilization and latency-compliant goodput. Use DistServe as conditioned mechanism evidence. Treat the 2026 concurrency preprint as a possible measured configuration, not an automatic API crossover.
4. **The tariff is a multidimensional contract.** A dated list-price table with model/snapshot, date/effectivity, USD, direct/cloud channel, region or processing scope, tier and context band. Explain input/output, reasoning-inclusive output, cache creation/read/lifetime/refresh, batch/priority, multimodal and tool/storage fees. Separate promotions and negotiated terms. Contrast matched content across tokenizers/languages without assuming a universal language multiplier.
5. **Three full workload models.** Each starts from a quality rubric and a finite terminal path ledger. Each reports API cost per call/trajectory, total cohort expense, accepted/unresolved counts and cost per accepted task, with explicit human time and tool overhead.
   - Short support: fixed policy question, no unauthorized promises/actions; inexpensive-first versus stronger-first routing, a bounded conditional fallback and human escalation. An assisted-support field study informs interpretation, not autonomous bot success probabilities.
   - Reusable documents: a stable prefix with dated query arrivals, source-grounded extraction/analysis, complete cache write/read/expiry costs, uncached and retrieval alternatives. Include parsing/indexing/validation assumptions; change reuse, churn, output and review.
   - Coding/research agent: a bounded change and acceptance after tests/review; per-step growing context, billed reasoning/output, tool costs, capped retry, stronger fallback, compaction and unresolved terminal state. Add a research-tool variant without pretending coding and research quality are identical.
6. **Sensitivity and API/self-hosting choices.** Show which variables dominate the three ledgers and quality-adjusted crossover conditions. A rented self-managed deployment will use a dated public quote if obtainable, otherwise a clearly hypothetical rental range coupled to a documented hardware/runtime configuration. Include whole-node billing, capacity and SLO limits, idle hours, reliability, operations, storage/network and quality parity. No invented benchmark will bridge mismatched GPUs or model variants.
7. **Who captures the value.** Hardware/infrastructure, model developer, application and customer map. Use disclosed financial/product evidence for what it actually measures. Analyze substitution, demand response, distribution, workflow integration and switching costs. Distinguish realized cash savings, time reallocation, willingness to pay, recognized revenue and profit. Give conditional procurement and product choices tied to the numerical break-even conditions.

Appendices will preserve source/version notes, formulas/units, complete path and cache ledgers, calculations, and narrow unresolved evidence issues. Main text will emphasize explanatory mechanisms and usable decisions rather than repeated disclaimers.
