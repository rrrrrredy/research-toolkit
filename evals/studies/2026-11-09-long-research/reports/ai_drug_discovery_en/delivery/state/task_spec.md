# Research brief

## question

Investigate what AI has demonstrably contributed to drug discovery and development, how representative programs have progressed, and which partnership structures connect computational work to experimental and clinical execution. Focus on traceable programs with public histories from 2018 through the evidence cutoff of 2026-11-09. Use earlier discoveries where needed to understand a program's origin. Only evidence publicly available by the cutoff may establish progress at that date; later trial updates must not be backdated.

Select a manageable set of identifiable programs with sufficient documentation to follow their history, using small-molecule discovery as the main comparison domain and including other modalities only when they illuminate a distinct mechanism. Do not preselect a fixed company roster or select only surviving success stories. Include discontinued, delayed, or inconclusive programs where reliable evidence is available. Explain the selection's limits rather than presenting it as a census of the industry.

Trace the role of AI through target identification or validation, molecular design and optimization, experimental screening, preclinical work, and clinical development. Distinguish an AI-assisted step from a claim that an entire discovery pipeline is AI-generated. Explain how computational proposals are tested, how experimental feedback changes the model or candidate selection, and which decisions remain biological, medicinal-chemical, operational, or clinical. Compare retrospective benchmark performance with prospective experimental results and downstream candidate progress.

For each principal program, resolve candidate identifiers and aliases, indication, target or proposed mechanism, sponsor, relevant partners, and dated development milestones. Cross-check company statements against publications, trial registries, regulatory records, and other primary materials where available. Distinguish a trial registration, first participant dosed, completed enrollment, readout, and regulatory decision. Track stage by candidate and indication rather than assigning one phase to all uses of a molecule. Explain conflicts or stale records without silently selecting the most favorable update.

Assess outcomes using the appropriate evidence for each stage. Discovery speed, assay performance, animal findings, safety observations, pharmacodynamic signals, and clinical efficacy are not interchangeable measures. Preserve trial design, sample size, comparator, endpoints, and disclosed limitations when interpreting clinical results. Do not attribute faster development or higher success probability to AI without an adequate comparison, and do not treat entry into a clinical trial as proof of therapeutic benefit. Where conventional-program comparisons are available, examine their selection and timing differences.

Analyze collaboration models among AI developers, biotechnology companies, pharmaceutical firms, and experimental or clinical service providers. Explain ownership of data and candidates, experimental responsibilities, decision rights, licensing scope, and development handoffs where disclosed. Separate upfront payments, contingent milestones, royalties, and the maximum announced deal value; an announced ceiling is not cash received. Connect partnership design to scientific feedback quality, execution risk, and incentives rather than using deal size as a proxy for validated drug performance.

Deliver a complete report with dated project evidence chains, a stage-aware comparison of results, and an analysis of partnership models and decision implications. The central checks are whether every progress claim refers to the correct candidate, indication, and date; whether the specific AI contribution is identifiable; and whether clinical conclusions respect the study design. Both useful narrow contributions and disappointing outcomes should remain visible. Recommendations may favor, limit, or defer a partnership or investment depending on evidence, without assuming either industry-wide transformation or industry-wide failure.
