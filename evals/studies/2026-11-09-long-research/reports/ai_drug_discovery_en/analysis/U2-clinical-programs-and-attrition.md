# Designed molecules and phenotypic repositioning: four different development outcomes

Author: Codex `/root/ttc_research`; author production, not independent review. Cutoff 2026-11-09.

## DSP-1181: a working design loop did not establish a useful medicine

DSP-1181 was developed by Sumitomo Dainippon Pharma, now Sumitomo Pharma, with Exscientia. It was intended initially for obsessive-compulsive disorder and described by the development scientists as a full serotonin 5-HT1A receptor agonist. It should not be merged with DSP-0038, another collaboration program with a different target profile and indication. The January 2020 announcement establishes that a Japanese phase I study had been initiated; it does not provide a patient efficacy result. In January 2022, Sumitomo reported discontinuing DSP-1181 because the phase I study had not met its expected criterion. The public explanation does not identify that criterion sufficiently to classify the termination specifically as toxicity, inadequate exposure or insufficient pharmacodynamic activity. [Initial announcement](https://www.sumitomo-pharma.com/news/20200130.html), [January 2022 management transcript, p. 12](https://www.sumitomo-pharma.com/ir/library/presentation/assets/pdf/etranscript20220131_01.pdf).

This remains one of the clearest descriptions of how computational and experimental work actually interacted. Exscientia generated structures and predicted activity, toxicity-related properties and pharmacokinetics. Sumitomo synthesized the proposed compounds and measured their properties; these observations were fed back into the predictive model. The team ran approximately two-week cycles. Medicinal chemists also made analogues using available synthetic intermediates, producing additional structure–activity information. When stronger binding failed to deliver the desired functional agonism after roughly three months, the team used matched molecular-pair analysis and complementary design strategies. The operational achievement was the ability to change the search direction when one objective stopped improving. [Scientists' technical account, pp. 3–4](https://www.sumitomo-chem.co.jp/english/rd/report/2021E_3.pdf).

DSP-1181 was the 350th compound synthesized in the project. The reported period of less than twelve months ended when a compound with the target profile was identified and **excluded safety evaluation**. The same account describes an already established collaboration spanning several projects. Comparing that clock directly with the entire conventional discovery and development process would attribute earlier platform-building, assay development and later safety work to different sides of the comparison. The useful claim is narrower and substantial: an integrated team carried out hundreds of design–make–test iterations quickly enough to nominate a candidate within the stated exploratory interval.

The program also used biological and translational techniques outside the design engine, including optogenetic disease-related experiments and biomarker work. These were attempts to connect receptor pharmacology with a clinically meaningful effect. They illustrate why a better predicted molecule is only one component of discovery. Functional agonism, brain exposure, tolerability, target engagement and improvement in the intended disorder must align. The termination preserves a valuable lesson for buyers: process capability can be demonstrated by a program that later fails, but the failure must remain in the platform's development record.

No numerical phase I success claim is assigned to DSP-1181 here. A trial identifier and detailed clinical dataset were not reliably established from the retrieved primary materials. The dated sponsor disclosure is sufficient to classify this program as discontinued after phase I assessment, but not to reconstruct its enrolled population, dose-response curve or exact reason for missing the criterion. A partnership assessment can use the documented discovery loop while requiring the clinical report before using DSP-1181 as evidence of safety optimization.

## EXS21546: target engagement and a therapeutic window are different achievements

EXS21546, also described as EVOEXS21546 and abbreviated ‘546, was Exscientia's A2A adenosine-receptor antagonist developed with Evotec. Its intended oncology role was to reverse adenosine-mediated immune suppression. The 2022 annual-report account describes candidate identification within nine months of novel design generation after 163 compounds were tested. Preclinical evidence included immune assays, tumor models and work with patient-derived material. This is evidence of computationally assisted molecular optimization against an established immunological hypothesis, not clinical evidence that patients' tumors respond. [Exscientia 2022 annual report, p. 90](https://d18rn0p25nwr6d.cloudfront.net/CIK-0001865408/2107e6d3-671e-4154-ba91-4e0c84f7bb7a.pdf), [AACR 2021 abstract](https://aacrjournals.org/cancerres/article/81/13_Supplement/1731/667605/Abstract-1731-EXS21546-a-non-CNS-penetrant-A2AR).

Two clinical records answer different questions. NCT04727138 was an early phase I study in healthy men, with 64 actual participants, beginning on 8 December 2020 and completing on 12 May 2022 according to the registry. It assessed safety, exposure and related pharmacology. NCT05920408, the phase Ib/IIa IGNITE study, concerned advanced solid tumors, including renal-cell carcinoma and non-small-cell lung cancer, and described combination treatment with the PD-1 inhibitor nivolumab. It began on 11 April 2023, enrolled six participants, and was terminated with an actual completion date of 23 October 2023. A healthy-volunteer phase I study and a patient combination study are not interchangeable entries in a clinical-success denominator. [Healthy-volunteer registry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04727138), [IGNITE registry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05920408).

The termination evidence is unusually instructive. Exscientia's annual report places the decision within a pipeline reprioritization and says the candidate was insufficiently promising to justify further development. The November 2023 registry update is more specific: emerging data made a suitable therapeutic index difficult to achieve. The two accounts can coexist, because portfolio selection often incorporates benefit–risk judgments. It would be inaccurate to classify the stop as an exclusively financial decision and then remove it from scientific attrition. It would also overstate the record to infer a specific dose-limiting toxicity or failed response-rate estimate without the underlying dataset. [2023 annual report, pipeline and risk-factor discussion](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1865408/000186540824000018/exai-20231231.htm).

Evotec's published account of its contribution identifies a concrete translational service: an ex vivo whole-blood assay measuring pCREB inhibition in CD8-positive T cells, adapted across preclinical and human samples and linked to plasma concentrations. Such a readout can establish pharmacological engagement and guide dose selection. The later oncology termination shows why engagement is an intermediate checkpoint. A drug can affect its intended pathway at measurable exposures and still lack a useful balance of activity and tolerability in the treatment setting. [Evotec target-engagement case study](https://www.evotec.com/sciencepool/translational-biomarkers-in-oncology).

The commercial arrangement also separates scientific roles. Aptuit, an Evotec affiliate, performed preclinical toxicology and manufacturing; costs for that arrangement were shared. The broader joint arrangement initially gave each party half of the relevant compound ownership, with Evotec's opt-out in 2021 reducing its interest as development progressed; the reported interest was 40% at the end of 2023, with a lower contractual floor at commercialization. This was risk-sharing around assets and experiments, rather than merely buying a software prediction. It also differs from Evotec's separate fee-for-service agreements. [Exscientia 2023 accounts, joint operations](https://s28.q4cdn.com/460399462/files/doc_financials/2023/ar/fy23-exs-plc-uk-ar_final.pdf).

## REC-994: a useful screening comparison followed by a negative development decision

REC-994 is tempol, a redox-cycling nitroxide and superoxide scavenger, repositioned for symptomatic cerebral cavernous malformation (CCM). Recursion sponsored the clinical program. Its origin is particularly useful because the 2015 academic discovery paper predates the modern platform branding and describes the actual learning method. Human endothelial cells with CCM2 knockdown were imaged; CellProfiler extracted morphological features, and CellProfiler Analyst learned to distinguish the perturbed and control states. Candidate compounds were ranked for phenotypic rescue. This is documented machine learning applied to experimental screening of an existing molecule, not de novo generation of tempol. [Original study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4356181/), [REC-994 clinical-pharmacology paper](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38655895/).

The primary screen tested 2,100 known drugs and bioactive compounds. Two blinded human reviewers agreed on 38 apparent rescuers; the automated system's top 38 compounds formed a separate comparison set. There was no overlap between these selections. In an orthogonal endothelial-function assay, seven machine-selected compounds showed full or partial rescue, compared with one human-selected compound. The selected-set yields were therefore 18.4% and 2.6%, a sevenfold ratio. This is a direct and useful comparison under the study's specific assay and selection conditions. It supports the proposition that quantitative image features can identify functionally relevant signals that visual inspection misses. It does not establish a sevenfold improvement over all conventional drug discovery or a sevenfold increase in clinical success.

The subsequent chain added vascular-leak experiments and chronic mouse studies. The abstract described roughly halved lesion burden for tempol and vitamin D3, but the detailed result matters: tempol's comparison with control was near the conventional significance threshold, with P approximately 0.052. This should be represented as supportive but uncertain animal evidence. It does not invalidate the screening comparison, which tested a different proposition. The eventual human trial tested whether the aggregate preclinical hypothesis translated into useful clinical activity.

SYCAMORE, NCT05085561, began on 17 March 2022. It randomized 62 patients to 200 mg REC-994, 400 mg REC-994 or placebo for a twelve-month controlled period, followed by an optional extension. In September 2024 the sponsor reported meeting the primary safety/tolerability objective and encouraging exploratory MRI trends at 400 mg. In May 2025 it announced that the trends had not persisted in the extension and that MRI and functional outcomes did not support continued development. This is the appropriate cutoff outcome: **development stopped after a safety-led phase II program with inadequate sustained evidence of benefit**. The initial safety readout remains true, but it cannot be used as a shorthand for clinical efficacy. [September 2024 release](https://ir.recursion.com/node/10581/pdf), [May 2025 sponsor statement distributed by Alliance to Cure](https://www.alliancetocure.org/statement-from-recursion-regarding-rec-994-trial/).

The registry adds a later public evidence layer that was available before this report's cutoff: results were first posted on **22 October 2026**. The controlled-period groups started with 21, 21 and 20 participants; 19, 20 and 18 completed that period. Several month-12 functional analyses had smaller available-data denominators. For example, the CCM Health Index least-squares mean changes were −5.8, −1.9 and −7.8 for the 200 mg, 400 mg and placebo groups, with 19, 16 and 17 participants analyzed. This does not display a coherent high-dose functional advantage. The record provides no inferential comparison for that table, so this report does not calculate a significance claim from the summary means. [SYCAMORE results registry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05085561).

The combined registry safety tables need special handling. Active-arm exposure combines the controlled period and extension, while the placebo arm contributes the controlled period only; crossover groups contribute extension exposure. The maximum follow-up is 1,201 days. Raw adverse-event counts in that table are not a duration-matched randomized safety comparison. Similarly, the registry's final administrative status of “completed” does not overturn the sponsor's May 2025 decision to discontinue development. Study closure and continuation of an asset are different facts.

REC-994 supports two conclusions simultaneously. Machine learning improved a specific experimental selection step compared with the study's human-selection benchmark. The resulting repositioning hypothesis did not produce a sufficiently persuasive sustained clinical signal to continue the program. The productive lesson is to invest in the first capability while placing a separate clinical evidence requirement on the second.

## REC-4881: promising clinical activity from a previously developed molecule

REC-4881 is TAK-733, a pre-existing allosteric MEK1/2 inhibitor licensed from Takeda by Recursion in May 2020. The principal program here is familial adenomatous polyposis (FAP), especially after colectomy. Recursion's phenotypic screening identified the compound's ability to reverse an APC-loss-associated cellular phenotype. The AI contribution is a disease–compound matching and biological-prioritization hypothesis. The platform did not originate TAK-733's chemical structure. This distinction also changes the meaning of its timeline: prior chemistry and human-development work were inherited with the asset. [Recursion 2025 annual report, REC-4881 discovery and Takeda license](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1601830/000160183026000039/rxrx-20251231.htm).

TUPELO, NCT05552755, is a nonrandomized, open-label phase I/II study; its registry lists an actual start on 10 July 2023 and recruiting status in the June 2026 public update. The estimated enrollment of 67 is a planned total, not the observed analysis population. The November 2026 clinical disclosures concern 4 mg once daily for twelve weeks followed by twelve weeks off treatment, with polyp burden defined as the sum of measured polyp diameters. Eligibility had expanded to adults aged 18 and older. [TUPELO registry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05552755), [2 November 2026 conference presentation](https://ir.recursion.com/static-files/e3eb390d-76f3-4899-bb92-922eaaaf3c30).

The reported results were encouraging: 13 of 15 patients with week-13 assessments had reduced total polyp burden, with a median reduction of 47%; 11 of 13 assessed at week 25 remained below baseline after twelve weeks off treatment. The safety population contained 26 patients at 4 mg, including five from phase Ib and 21 from phase II. Four had a highest treatment-related adverse-event grade of 3; none had grade 4 or 5. Four discontinued because of grade 1 or 2 treatment-related events. For a preventive treatment used in a chronic disease, those discontinuations matter even when severe events are uncommon. The sponsor anticipated a registrational study in the second half of 2027, subject to FDA discussions; that was a plan at the cutoff. [Dated results disclosure](https://ir.recursion.com/news-releases/news-release-details/new-phase-2-rec-4881-data-presented-cga-igc-show-deep-durable).

The denominators reveal what still needs to be demonstrated. Of 21 phase II patients at 4 mg, 16 met the efficacy-evaluable definition, which required measurable baseline disease, at least 75% drug exposure and a post-baseline endoscopy. Three lacked a post-baseline endoscopy and two had insufficient exposure. Fifteen contributed week-13 data and thirteen contributed week-25 data. Consequently, 87% is 13/15 among assessed patients, not a response rate in all 26 safety-evaluable patients or all 21 phase II participants. An evaluable analysis can reveal drug activity while favoring people able to remain on treatment and assessment. A confirmatory design should retain the whole assigned treatment population and prespecify handling of missing endoscopies and discontinuations.

The observation of activity after stopping treatment is biologically and clinically useful: it suggests a possible intermittent-treatment strategy worth testing. The presentations add back polyp burden excised at week 13 when calculating week-25 changes, addressing one obvious procedural source of apparent improvement. That correction does not create a concurrent control group. Baseline burden can be small, percentage changes can therefore be unstable, and measurement and intervention practices can differ across visits. The current evidence supports further development; it does not yet establish fewer cancers, fewer major surgeries or a validated long-term dosing schedule.

The natural-history comparison is context rather than a treatment-effect estimate. A retrospective subset of 47 older, post-colectomy patients from Amsterdam UMC was selected to resemble the original eligibility criteria. Routine-care endoscopies were usually annual, while TUPELO's first assessment was at week 13, and the clinical cohort later broadened in age. Differences in surveillance, anatomy, baseline burden, excisions and follow-up remain. These data strengthen the rationale that spontaneous sustained reduction is not the expected course for many patients, but they cannot quantify how much of the observed trial change is caused by REC-4881.

For diligence, the most valuable next deliverable is a prespecified, regulator-agreed study connecting polyp reduction to a clinically meaningful management benefit, with blinded or otherwise rigorously standardized assessment and a defensible comparator. The present result is a credible activity signal that justifies that investment. Its interpretation need not depend on calling the molecule AI-generated or claiming that its prior development history disappears.
