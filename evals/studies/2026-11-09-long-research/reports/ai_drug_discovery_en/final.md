# AI Drug Discovery: What the Programs, Trials and Partnerships Demonstrate

**Evidence cutoff: 9 November 2026**  
For pharmaceutical R&D, business-development and investment teams

AI has demonstrated practical value in drug discovery by helping researchers choose more informative experiments, identify overlooked biological relationships and optimize molecules through repeated laboratory feedback. Some of those contributions have led to clinical candidates and credible patient activity signals. The evidence is less mature for the broader claim that AI consistently increases the probability of a new medicine reaching approval or reduces the full cost of development.

The distinction is visible in seven identifiable programs. Rentosertib connects computational target prioritization and molecular design to preclinical experiments, a small randomized IPF trial and an ongoing oral phase III study. DSP-1181 documents a fast design–synthesis–assay loop but was discontinued after phase I assessment. EXS21546 generated translational target-engagement evidence before a small oncology study stopped with a therapeutic-index concern. REC-994 began with an unusually informative machine-versus-human screening comparison, then failed to sustain a persuasive clinical signal. REC-4881 has produced encouraging polyp-burden reductions in FAP, although the current study is small and uncontrolled. Baricitinib provides a clinically mature repurposing example, with randomized COVID-19 evidence and an indication-specific approval; its molecule and prior development were not created by the AI nomination. Halicin provides strong prospective screening evidence while remaining a preclinical antibacterial example in the verified record assembled here.

The commercial implication is to purchase the deliverable that has actually been demonstrated. A screening engagement can be justified by better experimental confirmation rates. A co-discovery relationship can be justified by a reliable design and laboratory loop. A clinical license requires evidence about the molecule's benefit–risk profile in the intended disease. Partner option exercises validate acceptance of a specified research package, while milestone ceilings describe contingent economics. Neither should substitute for the underlying experiments or clinical outcomes.

## 1. Scope and the unit of comparison

This report follows public histories from 2018 through the cutoff, using earlier discovery work when needed to establish origin. Small molecules are the common domain. The selection is deliberately limited to programs with traceable methods, milestones and outcomes, including stopped programs and boundary cases that clarify attribution. It is not a company ranking or an industry census.

The unit of comparison is the **candidate, indication and formulation at a specified date**. Oral rentosertib in IPF and an inhaled rentosertib formulation do not share a clinical stage automatically. TAK-733's earlier development and REC-4881's FAP program must be connected without treating the new indication as the invention of a new molecule. A phase I study in healthy volunteers cannot be counted as patient efficacy in cancer.

Four questions organize each history: what computational method changed a decision; what prospective experiment tested that proposal; what the clinical or regulatory record established; and which organization owned or executed the next step. Publications, registry records, regulatory notices and dated sponsor disclosures are used for their respective scopes. Where the same record contains both a measured outcome and a prediction about future development, the two are kept separate.

| Computational contribution | Experimental task it can improve | What still has to be demonstrated |
|---|---|---|
| Disease/target prioritization | Choose perturbations and targets for biological validation | Causal relevance in a disease context and a tractable therapeutic intervention |
| Molecular design and property prediction | Choose structures to synthesize and balance potency, exposure and liabilities | Measured pharmacology, manufacturability, safety and a useful therapeutic window |
| Phenotypic representation and screening | Detect rescue or relationships in high-dimensional cell measurements | Orthogonal functional confirmation and translation beyond the cell system |
| Knowledge integration and repurposing | Surface an actionable indication for an existing compound | Prospective clinical benefit in the new population and treatment setting |

## 3. Rentosertib: connecting target discovery, molecular design and clinical testing

### A target hypothesis became an experimentally tractable molecule

Rentosertib is the name used for the TNIK inhibitor previously described as INS018_055/INS018-055 and ISM001-055. The principal clinical program is oral treatment for idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF), sponsored by Insilico Medicine. The discovery account connects two computational jobs that are often conflated: selecting a disease-relevant target and designing a molecule against that target. PandaOmics integrated disease-associated molecular data with network and literature evidence; Chemistry42 proposed molecular structures against a specified binding-site hypothesis. The resulting molecule then passed through synthesis, biochemical assays, cellular experiments, animal studies and human exposure studies. This makes the program a substantive example of connected AI-assisted discovery. It does not make every experiment or development decision an AI output. [Discovery paper, published 8 March 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41587-024-02143-0).

The target selection was constrained in useful ways. TNIK ranked first after filters for protein/receptor kinases, novelty and small-molecule tractability. TNIK biology and structural information already existed. Its connections with WNT, TGF-β and other pathways supplied biological context, while the design team specified an ATP-binding pocket, a hinge interaction involving Cys108 and a hydrophobic region. AI helped prioritize an intervention and explore structures within that hypothesis. It did not establish that TNIK was the sole driver of IPF or that suppressing it would improve outcomes in patients.

Experimental feedback changed the candidate-selection problem. Early molecules had strong target activity but poor metabolic stability, CYP inhibition and low solubility. Lead optimization therefore had to improve exposure and drug-like properties, rather than maximize potency alone. The published account identifies these measured liabilities and the resulting candidate; it is less explicit about the contribution of each model update within every optimization cycle. A buyer of the platform should ask for the iteration history linking predicted properties, actual measurements and the next compound choices. The successful deliverable here is a molecule that could be made and tested across that sequence.

The evidence for biological plausibility is stronger than a docking score. The paper reports direct binding, biochemical kinase inhibition, concentration-dependent cellular effects and similarities between TNIK depletion and drug treatment in relevant signaling readouts. Primary fibroblasts from three IPF donors showed suppression of TGF-β-induced myofibroblast markers. These are useful orthogonal checks: a cellular phenotype alone might result from nonspecific toxicity, whereas genetic perturbation and several biochemical measurements help narrow the explanation. The kinase panel nevertheless identified activity against additional kinases. Rentosertib should be described as a TNIK inhibitor with a characterized activity profile, rather than as an exclusively TNIK-selective instrument proving a single-target mechanism.

Animal studies supplied a further bridge. Oral treatment reduced measures of fibrosis in bleomycin-injured mice; inhaled administration was separately examined in a rat model. These experiments provide support for antifibrotic activity under the tested conditions. They do not demonstrate superiority to established IPF medicines in patients. In particular, the injury model, treatment timing, dose and exposure differ from chronic human disease. The inhalation experiments also concern a different formulation and exposure strategy from the oral clinical program.

The frequently repeated discovery clock is approximately **18 months from target discovery to preclinical candidate nomination**. It is not 18 months to approval, and it does not incorporate the subsequent patient trials. The discovery paper additionally reports a placebo-controlled phase I study in 78 healthy participants, NCT05154240, and a separate Chinese phase I study. Those studies support the ability to deliver a clinical candidate and characterize short-term exposure. They answer a different question from efficacy in IPF.

### What the phase IIa trial established

The June 2025 publication reports a multicenter, double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled study of 71 adults with IPF at 21 Chinese sites. Participants received 30 mg once daily, 30 mg twice daily, 60 mg once daily, or placebo for 12 weeks; the active groups each contained 18 patients and placebo contained 17. Stable background nintedanib or pirfenidone was permitted. The primary objective was safety and tolerability, measured through treatment-emergent adverse events. Lung-function measures were secondary endpoints. [Phase IIa paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-025-03743-2), [NCT05938920](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05938920).

| Measure | 30 mg once daily | 30 mg twice daily | 60 mg once daily | Placebo |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Randomized | 18 | 18 | 18 | 17 |
| Completed trial | 16 | 12 | 12 | 15 |
| Any treatment-emergent adverse event | 13 | 15 | 15 | 12 |
| Treatment-related adverse event | 9 | 11 | 14 | 5 |

The safety counts come from the paper and its [original Table 2](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-025-03743-2/tables/2); completion and missing-measurement counts refer to different events and should not be substituted for one another. Twelve patients discontinued because of adverse events, while sixteen discontinued overall. Seven discontinuations involved liver injury or dysfunction, including four patients concurrently receiving nintedanib. That observation warrants specific interaction and exposure assessment; it does not establish that nintedanib caused the events or that rentosertib is free of liver liability when used alone.

The publication reports two distinct FVC analyses. Figure 2a summarizes participants with observed week-12 measurements; Figure 2b uses baseline-adjusted ANCOVA and multiple imputation under a missing-at-random assumption for the randomized groups. The registry's absolute-FVC outcome likewise separates the available-data means from the model-based least-squares means. These are different populations and estimators, not interchangeable presentations of one result. [Original Figure 2](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-025-03743-2/figures/2), [registered results](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05938920).

| FVC change at week 12 | 60 mg once daily | Placebo | Author subtraction of reported group point estimates |
|---|---|---|---|
| Observed cases, Figure 2a | n = 10; +98.4 mL (95% CI 10.9 to 185.9) | n = 14; −20.3 mL (95% CI −116.1 to 75.6) | 118.7 mL |
| ANCOVA with multiple imputation, Figure 2b | Randomized n = 18; +89.2 mL (95% CI −8.7 to 187.2) | Randomized n = 17; −8.2 mL (95% CI −98.1 to 81.7) | 97.4 mL |

Both analyses contain a favorable high-dose point estimate, but the observed-case result describes the people who contributed the measurement, while the imputed analysis extends to the randomized groups under its model assumptions. The high-dose group's modeled confidence interval includes zero. Neither subtraction above is a newly estimated between-group effect with a confidence interval; neither a within-group interval nor the overlap of separate group intervals is a valid substitute for the randomized treatment-contrast test.

Week-12 spirometry was missing for 22 of 71 participants, including 8 of 18 at the highest once-daily dose and 3 of 17 on placebo. Thus, 44.4% of the high-dose group lacked the measurement, compared with 17.6% of placebo. The observed-case analysis can be affected by which patients remained measurable; the full-group model depends on how unobserved outcomes are imputed. Discontinuation and missing lung-function measurements may relate to tolerability or clinical course, making missing-not-at-random sensitivity analyses important. These concerns do not erase the directionally favorable signal. They define what a larger, longer trial must resolve before the change can be interpreted as established clinical benefit.

Other outcomes reinforce that distinction. Quality-of-life results were largely inconclusive, and the paper reports three acute IPF exacerbations in the highest-dose group versus one on placebo. Small counts do not establish a reliable difference, but they belong in the benefit–risk assessment. Exploratory serum-protein changes support a pharmacodynamic account consistent with fibrosis-related pathways. They are neither an independently validated surrogate for patient benefit nor a substitute for sustained lung-function and clinical outcomes.

The sound development decision is to advance the clinical hypothesis while retaining explicit tests of its vulnerabilities: durability over a year, treatment-policy analyses after discontinuation, missing-not-at-random sensitivity analyses, liver monitoring and background-therapy interactions. A larger trial can convert the present mechanistic coherence and short-term signal into a clinically usable estimate. Its value will come from answering those questions, not simply from carrying a phase III label.

### Progress at the cutoff, with formulation and date preserved

The oral phase III study GENESIS-IPF-3, NCT07687459, was recruiting at the cutoff. Its registry lists an actual study start of **9 September 2026**, an estimated enrollment of **320**, and an annual FVC decline endpoint over **52 weeks**. The first public registry posting was **7 July 2026**; Insilico announced first-patient dosing on **10 September 2026**. July registration and the September dosing announcement are different milestones. No phase III efficacy result is established by these records. [Phase III registry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07687459), [first-dose announcement](https://insilico.com/news/isn1009261-insilico-medicine-doses-first-patient-genesis-ipf-3).

An April 2026 announcement concerns **IND clearance for an inhalation solution**, with a proposed phase I program in healthy participants and patients with IPF. It is a separate formulation-development branch. It should not inherit the oral program's phase III stage, and clearance to conduct a study is not evidence that enrollment or dosing occurred. The local-delivery rationale is credible: increasing pulmonary exposure relative to systemic exposure could change the therapeutic margin. The magnitude of that advantage in humans remains a clinical question. [Inhalation IND announcement, 28 April 2026](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/insilicos-rentosertib-inhalation-solution-receives-ind-clearance-for-the-worlds-first-ai-driven-candidate-to-enter-direct-to-lung-clinical-study-302755747.html).

## 4. Designed molecules and phenotypic repositioning: four development histories

### DSP-1181: a working design loop did not establish a useful medicine

DSP-1181 was developed by Sumitomo Dainippon Pharma, now Sumitomo Pharma, with Exscientia. It was intended initially for obsessive-compulsive disorder and described by the development scientists as a full serotonin 5-HT1A receptor agonist. It should not be merged with DSP-0038, another collaboration program with a different target profile and indication. The January 2020 announcement establishes that a Japanese phase I study had been initiated; it does not provide a patient efficacy result. In January 2022, Sumitomo reported discontinuing DSP-1181 because the phase I study had not met its expected criterion. The public explanation does not identify that criterion sufficiently to classify the termination specifically as toxicity, inadequate exposure or insufficient pharmacodynamic activity. [Initial announcement](https://www.sumitomo-pharma.com/news/20200130.html), [January 2022 management transcript, p. 12](https://www.sumitomo-pharma.com/ir/library/presentation/assets/pdf/etranscript20220131_01.pdf).

This remains one of the clearest descriptions of how computational and experimental work actually interacted. Exscientia generated structures and predicted activity, toxicity-related properties and pharmacokinetics. Sumitomo synthesized the proposed compounds and measured their properties; these observations were fed back into the predictive model. The team ran approximately two-week cycles. Medicinal chemists also made analogues using available synthetic intermediates, producing additional structure–activity information. When stronger binding failed to deliver the desired functional agonism after roughly three months, the team used matched molecular-pair analysis and complementary design strategies. The operational achievement was the ability to change the search direction when one objective stopped improving. [Scientists' technical account, pp. 3–4](https://www.sumitomo-chem.co.jp/english/rd/report/2021E_3.pdf).

DSP-1181 was the 350th compound synthesized in the project. The reported period of less than twelve months ended when a compound with the target profile was identified and **excluded safety evaluation**. The same account describes an already established collaboration spanning several projects. Comparing that clock directly with the entire conventional discovery and development process would attribute earlier platform-building, assay development and later safety work to different sides of the comparison. The useful claim is narrower and substantial: an integrated team synthesized and tested hundreds of compounds through repeated two-week feedback cycles, nominating a candidate within the stated exploratory interval.

The program also used biological and translational techniques outside the design engine, including optogenetic disease-related experiments and biomarker work. These were attempts to connect receptor pharmacology with a clinically meaningful effect. They illustrate why a better predicted molecule is only one component of discovery. Functional agonism, brain exposure, tolerability, target engagement and improvement in the intended disorder must align. The termination preserves a valuable lesson for buyers: process capability can be demonstrated by a program that later fails, but the failure must remain in the platform's development record.

No numerical phase I success claim is assigned to DSP-1181 here. A trial identifier and detailed clinical dataset were not reliably established from the retrieved primary materials. The dated sponsor disclosure is sufficient to classify this program as discontinued after phase I assessment, but not to reconstruct its enrolled population, dose-response curve or exact reason for missing the criterion. A partnership assessment can use the documented discovery loop while requiring the clinical report before using DSP-1181 as evidence of safety optimization.

### EXS21546: target engagement and a therapeutic window are different achievements

EXS21546, also described as EVOEXS21546 and abbreviated ‘546, was Exscientia's A2A adenosine-receptor antagonist developed with Evotec. Its intended oncology role was to reverse adenosine-mediated immune suppression. The 2022 annual-report account describes candidate identification within nine months of novel design generation after 163 compounds were tested. Preclinical evidence included immune assays, tumor models and work with patient-derived material. This is evidence of computationally assisted molecular optimization against an established immunological hypothesis, not clinical evidence that patients' tumors respond. [Exscientia 2022 annual report, p. 90](https://d18rn0p25nwr6d.cloudfront.net/CIK-0001865408/2107e6d3-671e-4154-ba91-4e0c84f7bb7a.pdf), [AACR 2021 abstract](https://aacrjournals.org/cancerres/article/81/13_Supplement/1731/667605/Abstract-1731-EXS21546-a-non-CNS-penetrant-A2AR).

Two clinical records answer different questions. NCT04727138 was an early phase I study in healthy men, with 64 actual participants, beginning on 8 December 2020 and completing on 12 May 2022 according to the registry. It assessed safety, exposure and related pharmacology. NCT05920408, the phase Ib/IIa IGNITE study, concerned advanced solid tumors, including renal-cell carcinoma and non-small-cell lung cancer, and described combination treatment with the PD-1 inhibitor nivolumab. It began on 11 April 2023, enrolled six participants, and was terminated with an actual completion date of 23 October 2023. A healthy-volunteer phase I study and a patient combination study are not interchangeable entries in a clinical-success denominator. [Healthy-volunteer registry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04727138), [IGNITE registry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05920408).

The termination evidence is unusually instructive. Exscientia's annual report places the decision within a pipeline reprioritization and says the candidate was insufficiently promising to justify further development. The November 2023 registry update is more specific: emerging data made a suitable therapeutic index difficult to achieve. The two accounts can coexist, because portfolio selection often incorporates benefit–risk judgments. It would be inaccurate to classify the stop as an exclusively financial decision and then remove it from scientific attrition. It would also overstate the record to infer a specific dose-limiting toxicity or failed response-rate estimate without the underlying dataset. [2023 annual report, pipeline and risk-factor discussion](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1865408/000186540824000018/exai-20231231.htm).

Evotec's published account of its contribution identifies a concrete translational service: an ex vivo whole-blood assay measuring pCREB inhibition in CD8-positive T cells, adapted across preclinical and human samples and linked to plasma concentrations. Such a readout can establish pharmacological engagement and guide dose selection. The later oncology termination shows why engagement is an intermediate checkpoint. A drug can affect its intended pathway at measurable exposures and still lack a useful balance of activity and tolerability in the treatment setting. [Evotec target-engagement case study](https://www.evotec.com/sciencepool/translational-biomarkers-in-oncology).

The commercial arrangement also separates scientific roles. Aptuit, an Evotec affiliate, performed preclinical toxicology and manufacturing; costs for that arrangement were shared. The broader joint arrangement initially gave each party half of the relevant compound ownership, with Evotec's opt-out in 2021 reducing its interest as development progressed; the reported interest was 40% at the end of 2023, with a lower contractual floor at commercialization. This was risk-sharing around assets and experiments, rather than merely buying a software prediction. It also differs from Evotec's separate fee-for-service agreements. [Exscientia 2023 accounts, joint operations](https://s28.q4cdn.com/460399462/files/doc_financials/2023/ar/fy23-exs-plc-uk-ar_final.pdf).

### REC-994: a useful screening comparison followed by a negative development decision

REC-994 is tempol, a redox-cycling nitroxide and superoxide scavenger, repositioned for symptomatic cerebral cavernous malformation (CCM). Recursion sponsored the clinical program. Its origin is particularly useful because the 2015 academic discovery paper predates the modern platform branding and describes the actual learning method. Human endothelial cells with CCM2 knockdown were imaged; CellProfiler extracted morphological features, and CellProfiler Analyst learned to distinguish the perturbed and control states. Candidate compounds were ranked for phenotypic rescue. This is documented machine learning applied to experimental screening of an existing molecule, not de novo generation of tempol. [Original study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4356181/), [REC-994 clinical-pharmacology paper](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38655895/).

The primary screen tested 2,100 known drugs and bioactive compounds. Two blinded human reviewers agreed on 38 apparent rescuers; the automated system's top 38 compounds formed a separate comparison set. There was no overlap between these selections. In an orthogonal endothelial-function assay, seven machine-selected compounds showed full or partial rescue, compared with one human-selected compound. The selected-set yields were therefore 18.4% and 2.6%, a sevenfold ratio. This is a direct and useful comparison under the study's specific assay and selection conditions. It supports the proposition that quantitative image features can identify functionally relevant signals that visual inspection misses. It does not establish a sevenfold improvement over all conventional drug discovery or a sevenfold increase in clinical success.

The subsequent chain added vascular-leak experiments and chronic mouse studies. The abstract described roughly halved lesion burden for tempol and vitamin D3, but the detailed result matters: tempol's comparison with control was near the conventional significance threshold, with P approximately 0.052. This should be represented as supportive but uncertain animal evidence. It does not invalidate the screening comparison, which tested a different proposition. The eventual human trial tested whether the aggregate preclinical hypothesis translated into useful clinical activity.

SYCAMORE, NCT05085561, began on 17 March 2022. It randomized 62 patients to 200 mg REC-994, 400 mg REC-994 or placebo for a twelve-month controlled period, followed by an optional extension. In September 2024 the sponsor reported meeting the primary safety/tolerability objective and encouraging exploratory MRI trends at 400 mg. In May 2025 it announced that the trends had not persisted in the extension and that MRI and functional outcomes did not support continued development. This is the appropriate cutoff outcome: **development stopped after a safety-led phase II program with inadequate sustained evidence of benefit**. The initial safety readout remains true, but it cannot be used as a shorthand for clinical efficacy. [September 2024 release](https://ir.recursion.com/node/10581/pdf), [May 2025 sponsor statement distributed by Alliance to Cure](https://www.alliancetocure.org/statement-from-recursion-regarding-rec-994-trial/).

The registry adds a later public evidence layer that was available before this report's cutoff: results were first posted on **22 October 2026**. The controlled-period groups started with 21, 21 and 20 participants; 19, 20 and 18 completed that period. Several month-12 functional analyses had smaller available-data denominators. For example, the CCM Health Index least-squares mean changes were −5.8, −1.9 and −7.8 for the 200 mg, 400 mg and placebo groups, with 19, 16 and 17 participants analyzed. This does not display a coherent high-dose functional advantage. The record provides no inferential comparison for that table, so this report does not calculate a significance claim from the summary means. [SYCAMORE results registry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05085561).

The combined registry safety tables need special handling. Active-arm exposure combines the controlled period and extension, while the placebo arm contributes the controlled period only; crossover groups contribute extension exposure. The maximum follow-up is 1,201 days. Raw adverse-event counts in that table are not a duration-matched randomized safety comparison. Similarly, the registry's final administrative status of “completed” does not overturn the sponsor's May 2025 decision to discontinue development. Study closure and continuation of an asset are different facts.

REC-994 supports two conclusions simultaneously. Machine learning improved a specific experimental selection step compared with the study's human-selection benchmark. The resulting repositioning hypothesis did not produce a sufficiently persuasive sustained clinical signal to continue the program. The productive lesson is to invest in the first capability while placing a separate clinical evidence requirement on the second.

### REC-4881: promising clinical activity from a previously developed molecule

REC-4881 is TAK-733, a pre-existing allosteric MEK1/2 inhibitor licensed from Takeda by Recursion in May 2020. The principal program here is familial adenomatous polyposis (FAP), especially after colectomy. Recursion's phenotypic screening identified the compound's ability to reverse an APC-loss-associated cellular phenotype. The AI contribution is a disease–compound matching and biological-prioritization hypothesis. The platform did not originate TAK-733's chemical structure. This distinction also changes the meaning of its timeline: prior chemistry and human-development work were inherited with the asset. [Recursion 2025 annual report, REC-4881 discovery and Takeda license](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1601830/000160183026000039/rxrx-20251231.htm).

TUPELO, NCT05552755, is a nonrandomized, open-label phase I/II study; its registry lists an actual start on 10 July 2023 and recruiting status in the June 2026 public update. The estimated enrollment of 67 is a planned total, not the observed analysis population. The November 2026 clinical disclosures concern 4 mg once daily for twelve weeks followed by twelve weeks off treatment, with polyp burden defined as the sum of measured polyp diameters. Eligibility had expanded to adults aged 18 and older. [TUPELO registry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05552755), [2 November 2026 conference presentation](https://ir.recursion.com/static-files/e3eb390d-76f3-4899-bb92-922eaaaf3c30).

The reported results were encouraging: 13 of 15 patients with week-13 assessments had reduced total polyp burden, with a median reduction of 47%; 11 of 13 assessed at week 25 remained below baseline after twelve weeks off treatment. The safety population contained 26 patients at 4 mg, including five from phase Ib and 21 from phase II. Four had a highest treatment-related adverse-event grade of 3; none had grade 4 or 5. Four discontinued because of grade 1 or 2 treatment-related events. For a preventive treatment used in a chronic disease, those discontinuations matter even when severe events are uncommon. The sponsor anticipated a registrational study in the second half of 2027, subject to FDA discussions; that was a plan at the cutoff. [Dated results disclosure](https://ir.recursion.com/news-releases/news-release-details/new-phase-2-rec-4881-data-presented-cga-igc-show-deep-durable).

The denominators reveal what still needs to be demonstrated. Of 21 phase II patients at 4 mg, 16 met the efficacy-evaluable definition, which required measurable baseline disease, at least 75% drug exposure and a post-baseline endoscopy. Three lacked a post-baseline endoscopy and two had insufficient exposure. Fifteen contributed week-13 data and thirteen contributed week-25 data. Consequently, 87% is 13/15 among assessed patients, not a response rate in all 26 safety-evaluable patients or all 21 phase II participants. An evaluable analysis can reveal drug activity while favoring people able to remain on treatment and assessment. A confirmatory design should retain the whole assigned treatment population and prespecify handling of missing endoscopies and discontinuations.

The observation of activity after stopping treatment is biologically and clinically useful: it suggests a possible intermittent-treatment strategy worth testing. The presentations add back polyp burden excised at week 13 when calculating week-25 changes, addressing one obvious procedural source of apparent improvement. That correction does not create a concurrent control group. Baseline burden can be small, percentage changes can therefore be unstable, and measurement and intervention practices can differ across visits. The current evidence supports further development; it does not yet establish fewer cancers, fewer major surgeries or a validated long-term dosing schedule.

The natural-history comparison is context rather than a treatment-effect estimate. A retrospective subset of 47 older, post-colectomy patients from Amsterdam UMC was selected to resemble the original eligibility criteria. Routine-care endoscopies were usually annual, while TUPELO's first assessment was at week 13, and the clinical cohort later broadened in age. Differences in surveillance, anatomy, baseline burden, excisions and follow-up remain. These data strengthen the rationale that spontaneous sustained reduction is not the expected course for many patients, but they cannot quantify how much of the observed trial change is caused by REC-4881.

For diligence, the most valuable next deliverable is a prespecified, regulator-agreed study connecting polyp reduction to a clinically meaningful management benefit, with blinded or otherwise rigorously standardized assessment and a defensible comparator. The present result is a credible activity signal that justifies that investment. Its interpretation need not depend on calling the molecule AI-generated or claiming that its prior development history disappears.

## 5. Repurposing and prospective selection: baricitinib and halicin

### Baricitinib: a narrow computational contribution followed by strong clinical evidence

Baricitinib, marketed as Olumiant by Lilly in partnership with Incyte, was already an anti-inflammatory medicine when BenevolentAI and academic collaborators proposed it for COVID-19. The February 2020 hypothesis was a repurposing proposal for an existing molecule. Machine learning helped extract and organize biomedical relationships; expert queries connected viral-entry pathways and drug pharmacology. The proposal emphasized AAK1 and related endocytosis biology alongside the drug's JAK-mediated anti-inflammatory activity. The molecule's original chemistry, manufacturing, prior safety database and marketed status predated this computational exercise. [Original correspondence, online 4 February 2020, subsequently corrected](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7137985/).

The later methodological account is explicit about human involvement. Researchers augmented the knowledge graph with coronavirus-related information, used interactive graph queries and protein-network analysis, and applied expert judgment about mechanism, exposure and suitability. This is a defensible AI contribution because it makes a large body of relationships more searchable and helps researchers identify a tractable hypothesis rapidly. It is not evidence that a model independently discovered every relevant connection or that an autonomous system selected the final treatment. [BenevolentAI workflow paper](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/pharmacology/articles/10.3389/fphar.2021.709856/full).

The program then acquired unusually strong downstream evidence for an AI-associated repurposing case. Those results should be credited to the drug in the studied clinical settings and to the clinical organizations that tested it. They do not, by themselves, identify the fraction of benefit caused by any proposed antiviral mechanism, nor estimate how much faster the development would have proceeded without the knowledge graph. The sequence demonstrates a useful computational nomination followed by independent clinical testing, which is a stronger and more precise claim than saying AI created a COVID-19 medicine from scratch.

Sources: [ACTT-2 full paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7745180/), [COV-BARRIER full paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8409066/), [RECOVERY full paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9333998/).

This sequence is valuable precisely because it contains a mixed result rather than an uninterrupted series of positive headlines. ACTT-2 supported faster recovery in its treatment setting. COV-BARRIER did not meet its primary progression endpoint; the prespecified testing hierarchy means that its mortality P value is nominal rather than protected by the original multiplicity procedure. RECOVERY subsequently supplied a much larger randomized mortality comparison. Its open-label design matters for some subjective or care-dependent outcomes, but mortality is less vulnerable to expectation effects than a subjective symptom score. Together the studies give a stronger basis for benefit than any single exploratory result.

Differences between the trials are informative. ACTT-2 enrolled from May to July 2020, when routine use of corticosteroids for COVID-19 had not become the later standard. COV-BARRIER and RECOVERY tested baricitinib amid much more frequent corticosteroid use; some RECOVERY participants also received tocilizumab. Baseline severity, calendar period and co-interventions affect absolute benefits. From the reported raw counts, the absolute mortality difference is approximately 5.03 percentage points in COV-BARRIER and 1.23 points in RECOVERY. These are author calculations on raw proportions, not the adjusted hazard or rate ratios reported by the trials. They should not be used to rank trial quality or claim that a biological effect diminished by a particular amount.

The regulatory milestones are separate from those readouts. FDA first authorized baricitinib with remdesivir under an emergency-use authorization in November 2020. It revised that authorization in July 2021 to permit standalone treatment. In May 2022 it approved the COVID-19 indication for hospitalized adults requiring supplemental oxygen, non-invasive or invasive mechanical ventilation, or extracorporeal membrane oxygenation. These are indication-specific decisions, not a blanket endorsement for every stage of infection or every age group. [FDA's dated approval notice](https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/USFDA/bulletins/31738e6).

The collaboration mechanism differs from the asset-licensing examples elsewhere in this report. BenevolentAI contributed a hypothesis and supporting analytical workflow; Lilly and Incyte supplied the existing medicine and development capabilities; NIAID and multinational clinical sites conducted ACTT-2, and the RECOVERY network supplied a major independent comparison. The ACTT-2 paper identifies investigator-and-sponsor protocol development with Lilly input, site collection and analysis by the statistical center and sponsor. The public materials reviewed do not establish a royalty-bearing transfer of baricitinib rights to BenevolentAI. Scientific contribution should therefore not be translated into assumed asset ownership or commercial participation.

For a pharmaceutical team, this is a strong argument for using computational knowledge integration when the action space contains already characterized drugs and a rapid prospective trial is possible. Much of the speed comes from that starting position: the molecule exists, doses and manufacturing are known, and prior human exposure reduces some uncertainties. That is an advantage of repurposing, not a reason to compare its calendar directly with discovery of a new molecule against a new target. The value proposition is earlier identification of a testable opportunity and more efficient use of existing pharmacology.

### Halicin: prospective experimental enrichment without clinical validation

Halicin is SU3327, an existing compound previously studied as a JNK inhibitor, identified for antibacterial activity by an academic team led from MIT and collaborators. The 2020 Cell paper is an unusually transparent account of a predictive model being taken beyond a retrospective benchmark. The model was trained on experimental growth-inhibition measurements for 2,335 unique compounds against E. coli. It then ranked compounds in a separate repurposing library. The molecular structure was not newly generated by the model. The specific contribution was predicting an antibacterial activity that was not the compound's original intended use. [Original Cell paper, including methods and supplemental figures](https://people.csail.mit.edu/tommi/papers/Stokes_etal_Cell2020.pdf).

The model combined a directed message-passing representation of molecular structure with calculated chemical features and an ensemble. Its reported test-set ROC-AUC was 0.896. That benchmark measures how well scores separate labels within a particular test distribution. The more consequential evidence came next: of 99 highly ranked compounds unique to the Drug Repurposing Hub, 51 inhibited E. coli under the follow-up assay criterion; among 63 low-ranked unique compounds, two did. The corresponding selected-set yields are 51.5% and 3.2%. This prospective enrichment shows that the ranking carried useful experimental information. It does not estimate sensitivity across the entire chemical universe, because most compounds were not assayed and the two tested groups were selected from the score extremes.

Researchers then applied additional filters, including structural dissimilarity and predicted toxicity, to choose halicin for deeper work. Antibacterial assays, resistance panels and mechanistic experiments followed. The growth predictor was agnostic about mechanism; subsequent experiments supported dissipation of the pH component of the bacterial proton motive force. The mechanistic result came from laboratory work after selection. It should not be retroactively described as a mechanism accurately predicted by the original classifier.

The often repeated claim about searching more than 107 million molecules belongs to a **different branch of the study**. After work on the repurposing library, the team scored 107,349,233 ZINC15 molecules. Twenty-three selected compounds meeting score and structural-dissimilarity criteria were obtained for experimental testing; eight inhibited at least one tested bacterial species. Halicin was not generated from that 107 million-molecule search, and eight positives among 23 selected compounds is not a validation of 107 million predicted labels. It demonstrates that virtual screening can direct scarce experimental effort toward chemically distinctive candidates at a scale that direct exhaustive testing could not practically match.

The preclinical evidence was substantial but bounded. Halicin showed activity against several pathogens and resistant isolates, but the tested Pseudomonas aeruginosa isolates were an important limitation. The authors were unable to obtain resistant mutants under their specified serial-passage and solid-medium experiments; this supports further interest in the mechanism, rather than establishing that resistance can never emerge. Mouse experiments included topical treatment of an A. baumannii wound model and oral treatment in a C. difficile infection model. Routes and infection sites matter: a topical bacterial-load reduction does not establish safe systemic exposure for human bloodstream or pulmonary infection.

The original methods also make the experimental denominator visible. The C. difficile experiment randomized mice to halicin, metronidazole or vehicle, but three mice assigned to halicin did not establish colonization. The wound model was not blinded. Those details do not negate activity; they prevent a small exploratory animal experiment from being treated as a mature clinical development package. The record assembled here establishes preclinical antibacterial findings, not a human efficacy result or regulatory approval for halicin as an antibiotic.

Halicin is therefore a persuasive case for funding prospective screening and medicinal-chemistry follow-up. Its next investment gate should concern exposure, toxicity, formulation, resistance behavior under relevant conditions and a development path for a specific infection. An antibiotics company could value the model's ability to surface unconventional chemical matter while declining to license a particular molecule until those properties are established. This separation is central to evaluating AI-enabled discovery: useful selection technology and a developable drug candidate are related but independently testable products.

## 6. Stage-aware comparison: what the selected programs support

The most consistent benefit across these cases is improved selection under experimental constraints. Molecular-design systems can propose compounds that address several properties at once; image-based models can find phenotypic relationships that are difficult to recognize visually; knowledge graphs can make a repurposing hypothesis easier to identify and investigate. The value appears when an experiment changes the next decision. A larger generated library, a higher retrospective score or a more elaborate platform description is useful only insofar as it improves that sequence.

The cases expose three distinct kinds of feedback. **Model feedback** updates a predictor using new assay measurements, as described in DSP-1181's design loop. **Selection feedback** changes which candidate is synthesized or advanced, even where a paper does not disclose whether the model itself was retrained. **Biological feedback** changes the therapeutic hypothesis, for example when cellular rescue fails to produce a sustained patient benefit. A platform may be effective at the first two while remaining vulnerable to the third. Contracts and investment milestones should recognize all three.

| Decision being tested | Stronger evidence in this selection | Appropriate interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Can a ranking improve experimental selection? | Halicin's prospective high-score/low-score assays; the original CCM screen's machine-versus-human selection comparison | Useful enrichment within the tested library and assay |
| Can an iterative design system produce a clinical candidate? | DSP-1181, EXS21546 and rentosertib | Feasible design, synthesis, optimization and development handoff |
| Does a pharmacological hypothesis act in humans? | EXS21546 target-engagement work; rentosertib exploratory biomarkers | Mechanism or exposure support, with therapeutic-window and efficacy questions remaining |
| Is there a promising patient activity signal? | Rentosertib phase IIa FVC; REC-4881 polyp burden | Rationale for better powered or more controlled development, tailored to the signal's limitations |
| Is there evidence of patient benefit in a defined indication? | Multiple randomized baricitinib COVID-19 trials and an adult regulatory approval | Benefit of that drug in specified populations and treatment settings |
| Does AI increase end-to-end industry productivity? | No matched causal comparison in this seven-program selection | Requires a broader cohort, common clocks and complete attrition accounting |

This comparison avoids forcing unrelated endpoints onto one success scale. A discovery hit rate and a mortality reduction are both valuable, but they are different outputs with different denominators. The strong clinical evidence for an existing repurposed drug does not establish that de novo generation has solved clinical efficacy. Equally, a discontinued clinical candidate does not erase a controlled demonstration that a screening method selected more functional hits.

The selected programs are intentionally heterogeneous in origin and outcome. Three new-molecule programs show different clinical trajectories; two phenotypic-repositioning programs include both discontinuation and promising activity; an approved-drug repurposing case supplies a clinically mature boundary; and an academic antibacterial lead supplies a prospective screening boundary. This is a mechanism-oriented sample selected for traceability, not a census or a denominator for estimating industry success. Three stopped programs out of seven selected cases would be an artifact of selection, not an industry failure rate.

### How to interpret speed and success-rate comparisons

Discovery clocks must start and stop at comparable events. DSP-1181's interval excludes safety evaluation; rentosertib's ends at preclinical candidate nomination. An inherited clinical compound such as TAK-733 begins its new-indication program with information unavailable to an entirely new chemical series. Baricitinib starts with an existing drug and human safety experience. A fair comparison would distinguish target selection, hit identification, lead optimization, candidate nomination, IND-enabling work and first dosing, while including the programs that never reached the next milestone.

The strongest conventional comparison retained here is local: the CCM study held the initial screen and selected-set sizes constant when comparing machine and human prioritization. Its functional confirmation assay supplied an outcome beyond the image classification task. A useful platform evaluation could generalize that design across prospectively chosen projects, equal laboratory budgets and predefined nomination criteria. By contrast, comparing a selected successful program's short exploratory interval with an historical industry average leaves target difficulty, prior work, staffing, assay readiness and failure selection unresolved.

One published early industry analysis reported 21 successes among 24 completed phase I programs and four among ten phase II programs, using an AI-native-company cohort and excluding COVID-19-related molecules. Its definitions encompassed several modes of AI contribution. These were small, historical, observational denominators; the paper itself describes incomplete coverage and classification limitations. The figures should not be presented as current November 2026 success rates or as a matched estimate of AI's causal benefit. [Jayatunga and colleagues, 2024; author-uploaded full text](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/380223979_How_successful_are_AI-discovered_drugs_in_clinical_trials_A_first_analysis_and_emerging_lessons).

Author calculations illustrate the sampling uncertainty without pretending to fix the cohort design. The point estimates are 87.5% and 40%; approximate 95% Wilson intervals are 69.0–95.7% and 16.8–68.7%, respectively. These intervals condition on the published labels and assume binomial sampling. They do not correct incomplete disclosure, different follow-up, correlated programs, selection of companies, or classification of business-related stops. A completed-outcome cohort can also overrepresent programs that read out quickly. What remains useful is a hypothesis to test: better molecular property optimization may improve early development performance. Establishing that hypothesis requires appropriate comparators and continuing follow-up.

The distinction between scientific and commercial stopping decisions also needs care. If a candidate has insufficient differentiation or a narrow therapeutic index, reallocating funds is both a portfolio decision and an assessment of its scientific prospects. EXS21546 is a concrete example. Excluding every “reprioritized” program from attrition would favor promotional explanations over the underlying clinical record. Conversely, a financing-driven stop without negative data is not evidence of biological failure. A usable cohort should retain the program, record the disclosed reason and separate uncertainty from confirmed failure.

## 7. Partnership structures, rights and economics

The partnerships represented here buy different things: an operating discovery loop, shared candidate ownership, rights to an existing molecule, a development candidate ready for handoff, or access to a reusable experimental map. Their economics cannot be compared without specifying the deliverable.

| Model | Concrete example | Scientific and operational allocation | Diligence implication |
|---|---|---|---|
| Joint discovery with a pharmaceutical laboratory | Sumitomo–Exscientia / DSP-1181 | Computational proposals and predictions combined with rapid synthesis, assays and medicinal-chemistry additions | Evaluate cycle time, assay quality and the fate of failed predictions, not only candidate count |
| Shared asset development and laboratory services | Exscientia–Evotec / EXS21546 | Compound interests and shared risk coexist with specific toxicology, manufacturing and assay work | Keep co-ownership terms separate from fee-for-service terms; determine who funds the next clinical step |
| In-license an existing molecule for a new disease | Takeda–Recursion / TAK-733→REC-4881 | Recursion acquires development rights and bears the new program's execution risk | Inspect inherited toxicology, exposure, manufacturing and IP package alongside the new disease hypothesis |
| Candidate option and pharmaceutical handoff | Exscientia/Recursion–Sanofi | Discovery organization delivers an accepted candidate; pharma leads downstream development | Define the acceptance package and access to later clinical feedback |
| Experimental maps, data access and program options | Recursion–Roche/Genentech | Shared inference and validation; distinct rights to maps, raw images and selected programs | Data-use rights can be economically separate from molecule rights |
| Public/private repurposing execution | Baricitinib | Computational hypothesis, existing drug capabilities and large clinical networks | Scientific credit does not establish ownership of the underlying medicine |

The **Takeda license** shows how an AI-associated program can rest on conventional asset transfer. The disclosed May 2020 agreement granted Recursion exclusive worldwide development, manufacturing and commercialization rights to TAK-733, with a nonclinical research license retained by Takeda. Recursion paid $1.5 million upfront and could owe up to $39.5 million in development/regulatory milestones plus low-to-mid single-digit sales royalties. The scope and obligations attach to the licensed compound and know-how, not to a general claim of AI performance. [Takeda license summary in Recursion's 2025 annual report](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1601830/000160183026000039/rxrx-20251231.htm).

For REC-4881, this structure can be efficient because a previously developed molecule provides a practical starting point for testing the APC–MEK hypothesis. It also transfers obligations that a screening result alone does not solve: manufacturing continuity, dose selection, chronic tolerability and a regulatory strategy for FAP. The low upfront relative to a large discovery-platform headline is not evidence of inferior science. It prices a different asset at a different point in uncertainty and leaves substantial future execution with the licensee.

The **Sanofi collaboration** makes the handoff explicit. The initial 2022 terms contemplated up to 15 programs: $100 million upfront, approximately $343 million of potential milestones per program and an approximately $5.2 billion aggregate milestone ceiling, with sales royalties. Exscientia led design, translational and early preclinical work; after candidate selection, Sanofi took responsibility for IND-enabling work, clinical development, manufacturing and commercialization at its expense. A joint steering committee oversaw research. [Filed January 2022 agreement summary](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1865408/000110465922002006/tm222148d1_6k.htm).

The filed contract distinguishes platform and program rights. Exscientia retains its platform technology IP and is not required to disclose how the platform operates. Sanofi receives broad program-related licenses, while Exscientia's use of Sanofi-provided data is restricted to the purposes of the applicable research plan. Some ownership allocations are redacted; an exclusive license does not prove outright ownership of every source dataset or future invention. These disclosed boundaries matter for learning: a company can retain its model technology while being unable to reuse partner data freely across unrelated projects. [Filed contract, clauses 20–23](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1865408/000186540822000005/exscientia-sanoficollabora.htm).

An October 2026 event makes the handoff concrete. Sanofi optioned the collaboration's first development candidate, an oral dual-target immunology molecule whose public name and targets were not disclosed in the reviewed release. The option triggered a $10 million milestone; total announced upfront and milestone payments under the collaboration reached $144 million. Sanofi was to fund and lead further development, with phase I planned for 2027. This establishes partner acceptance and a downstream commitment, not human efficacy or even completed phase I entry by the cutoff. [15 October 2026 announcement](https://ir.recursion.com/news-releases/news-release-details/recursion-announces-sanofi-options-potential-first-class).

The cash categories are important. Multiplying the initial per-program ceiling by 15 gives about $5.145 billion, consistent with the rounded $5.2 billion announcement; it does not imply all programs will begin or all milestones will be achieved. The $100 million upfront is about 1.9% of upfront plus the announced milestone ceiling. That ratio is descriptive, not an estimated probability of success. The later $144 million total includes earlier payments and is not $144 million earned by the newly optioned candidate alone. Cash received, a milestone triggered, accounting revenue and the maximum amount contractually available are four different measures. The October release states a specific revenue period for the $10 million milestone; it does not justify recognizing the remaining ceiling as revenue.

The **Roche/Genentech collaboration** separates reusable data from program rights. The 2021 agreement provided for Recursion to build phenotypic maps, with specified querying/access rights, separate options for external use of certain raw images, and options on validated targets or small-molecule programs in defined neuroscience and gastrointestinal-oncology fields. It contemplated up to 40 small-molecule programs, potentially more than $300 million in milestones per successful optioned program plus royalties. The $150 million upfront was received in January 2022. [Original filed agreement summary](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1601830/000119312521349657/d242203d8k.htm), [dated receipt disclosure](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1601830/000160183022000012/R26.htm).

In August 2026, the companies described advancing a first neuroscience target after pathway, functional and disease-model validation. The next stage was joint small-molecule hit generation and validation. This is a traceable deliverable from the mapping relationship, although the public account does not disclose a target identity that would permit an independent candidate-level clinical history. It should be kept separate from named clinical assets such as REC-4881. [First neuroscience-target account](https://recursion.com/news/unlocking-the-first-neuroscience-target-for-the-recursion-and-genentech-collaboration).

That structure makes scientific sense when the initial product is a map capable of generating several hypotheses. Payment for data access or map acceptance rewards an earlier deliverable than a candidate license. It can support expensive assay and cell-production work before a molecule exists. It also creates an incentive question: acceptance criteria must test biological usefulness and reproducibility, rather than merely data volume. A map can be technically impressive while contributing few tractable targets. Conversely, a useful target can justify the collaboration even before any clinical outcome is available, provided its value is priced at that stage.

## 8. Decisions for R&D, business-development and investment teams

For a **screening or design engagement**, use an observable prospective deliverable. Freeze a set of tasks, comparator methods, laboratory resources and acceptance criteria before prediction. Include negative measurements and failed syntheses in the returned dataset. Measure confirmation rates, property trade-offs and time to a usable decision. The CCM and halicin experiments show why an orthogonal assay is especially useful: it checks whether a visually or statistically attractive prediction survives a different biological measurement.

For **co-discovery**, assess the physical feedback loop as carefully as the model. DSP-1181's short cycles depended on chemists who could make compounds quickly and assays that could measure the properties relevant to the next choice. A model provider without access to comparable experiments may not reproduce the same speed. Specify who can change the target product profile, who chooses compounds when predictions disagree with chemistry, and how uncertain or failed measurements are retained. These decisions determine whether the project learns or simply generates more candidates.

For a **clinical asset license**, evaluate the molecule in its indication and formulation. Rentosertib merits attention because the target-to-clinic chain and randomized signal are documented, while the next decision depends on longer-term efficacy, missing-data robustness and liver safety. REC-4881 merits further development because substantial polyp reductions persist in some patients off treatment, while the next gate concerns a credible controlled or otherwise regulator-accepted comparison and clinical relevance. DSP-1181, EXS21546 and REC-994 do not support a positive continuation case on the evidence reviewed; any revival would need a specific new rationale addressing the earlier stop.

For **platform investment**, separate recurring scientific capability from the success of one asset. Request a complete dated history of accepted and rejected programs, including unsuccessful designs and discontinued assets. Distinguish prospective evidence generated after a frozen prediction from retrospective examples selected after a successful result. Treat partner options and milestone payments as evidence that another organization accepted a defined deliverable. Their meaning strengthens when the deliverable can be inspected and linked to an experiment; it does not automatically extend to clinical benefit.

The most defensible investment thesis is specific: computational methods can improve which experiments are performed and how their results guide the next decision. The most consequential remaining uncertainty is how often those gains survive human biology, therapeutic-window constraints and clinical execution. The seven histories show both sides with enough specificity to structure a useful partnership: pay for the evidence already produced, assign responsibility for the next uncertainty, and preserve access to the feedback that will resolve it.
