The present deliverable is the research architecture, initial evidence analysis, and executable division of work—not the final report. Actual web searches and primary-source reading have been completed. No new benchmark run, independent reproduction, delegated execution, or independent review is claimed.\n\nScope: publicly available definitions, artifacts, and representative results from 2022 through November 9, 2026, with earlier MMLU and HumanEval material used only to establish definitions. Six comparison families keep the study manageable: (1) HELM Classic, Lite, and Capabilities; (2) MMLU/MMLU-Pro, including their implementation through lm-evaluation-harness; (3) HumanEval/HumanEval+ through EvalPlus; (4) repository-level evaluation through SWE-bench, Verified, and separately identified SWE-Bench Pro revisions; (5) τ-bench and τ²-bench, with the 2026 repository revisions treated as distinct evaluation contracts; and (6) human preference through Chatbot Arena, contrasted with MT-Bench and AlpacaEval. LiveCodeBench supplies a focused refresh case, and HELMET supplies grounding/long-context evidence rather than another full leaderboard comparison. Voice and multimodal performance are outside the principal comparison, but version changes involving them must not be silently attributed to text evaluations. There is no geographic restriction: language, cultural, domain, and repository coverage will be recorded explicitly rather than assuming English or public Python repositories represent global workloads.\n\nPROPOSED REPORT STRUCTURE\n1. What decision is being made, and what exactly has been evaluated?\n   Define a benchmark dataset, evaluation framework, execution harness, and leaderboard as different objects that can coexist within one project. HELM as the entry point: a framework is not one immutable benchmark.\n   Reconstruct the original multi-metric design, sampling, adaptation, and transparency provisions. Explain the later scope changes in Lite and Capabilities, including omitted measurements and changed aggregation. Connect official definitions to actual adapter code. Address maintenance and external-service dependencies without equating available source code with continuing executability.\n\n3. SWE-bench versus Verified: explain why scores on differently filtered task sets are not a model-improvement time series; separate model, scaffold, retrieval, and denominator effects.\n   D. τ evaluations: explain pass@k versus pass^k, domain versus task weighting, simulator dependence, and score changes from regrading existing trajectories.\n   E. Examine flawed reference answers, insufficient or over-restrictive tests, benchmark refreshes, hidden tests, evaluator position/verbosity/self-preference effects, population selection, finite samples, task clustering, repeated attempts, and multiple comparisons. Deployment decision I: grounded customer support.\n   Map the evidence to retrieval, evidence use, citation support, policy compliance, clarification, escalation, abstention, and authorized tool actions. Recommendations remain conditional on the organization's workload and risk limits.\n\n8.
