P34L45:

P34L53:

P36L3: Fact Sheet 4: Pressure Drop and Controlling System Pressure 
P36L4:  
P36L5: Pressure drop is a term used to characterize the 
P36L6: reduction in air pressure from the compressor discharge 
P36L7: to the actual point-of-use. Pressure drop occurs in the 
P36L8: piping as the compressed air travels through the 
P36L9: treatment and distribution system. A properly designed 
P36L10: system should have a pressure loss of much less than 10 
P36L11: percent of the compressor’s discharge pressure, 
P36L12: measured from compressor discharge output to the end 
P36L13: point-of-use. 
P36L14:  
P36L15: Excessive pressure drop will result in poor system 
P36L16: performance and excessive energy consumption. 
P36L17: Pressure drop upstream of the compressor signal 
P36L18: requires higher compression pressures to achieve the 
P36L19: control settings on the compressor.  Control specialists 
P36L20: should be consulted for these applications.  Flow 
P36L21: restrictions of any type require higher operating 
P36L22: pressures than necessary, and minimizing differentials 
P36L23: in all parts of the system is an important part of 
P36L24: efficient operation. The most typical problem areas 
P36L25: include the aftercooler, lubricant separators, air 
P36L26: treatment equipment, and check valves. A rule of 
P36L27: thumb for systems in the 100 psig range is: for every 2 
P36L28: psi increase in discharge pressure, energy consumption 
P36L29: will increase by approximately 1 percent at full output 
P36L30: flow (check performance curves for centrifugal and two-
P36L31: stage, lubricant-injected, rotary screw compressors). 
P36L32:  
P36L33: There is an additional penalty for higher-than-needed 
P36L34: pressure. Elevated system pressure increases 
P36L35: unregulated uses, such as leaks, open blowing, and 
P36L36: production applications. The added demand at elevated 
P36L37: pressure is termed “artificial demand” and can 
P36L38: substantially increase energy consumption. Although it 
P36L39: varies by plant, unregulated usage is commonly as high 
P36L40: as 30 to 50 percent of air demand. For systems in the 
P36L41: 100 psig range  a 2 psi increase in header pressure will 
P36L42: increase energy consumption  another 0.6 to 1.0 
P36L43: percent because of the additional unregulated air being 
P36L44: consumed. The combined effect results in a total 
P36L45: increase in energy consumption of about 1.6 to 2 
P36L46: percent for every 2 psi increase in discharge pressure. 
P36L47:  
P36L48: Pressure drop in the distribution system and in hoses 
P36L49: and flexible connections at points-of-use results in 
P36L50: lower operating pressure to the user. If the point-of- 
P36L51: use operating pressure has to be increased, try reducing 
P36L52: the pressure drops by properly sizing components or 
P36L53: adding secondary storage in the system before adding 
P36L54: capacity or increasing the system pressure. Increasing 
P36L55: the compressor discharge pressure or adding 
P36L56: compressor capacity may result in significant increases 
P36L57: in energy consumption and should be the last resort. 
P36L58:  
P36L59: What Causes Pressure Drop? 
P36L60: Any type of obstruction, restriction, or roughness in the 
P36L61: system will cause resistance to air flow and cause 
P36L62: pressure drop. In the distribution system, the highest 
P36L63: pressure drops usually are found at the points- of-use, 
P36L64: including undersized or leaking hoses, tubes, 
P36L65: disconnects, filters, regulators and lubricators (FRLs) 
P36L66: usually within the last 30 feet of the system. On the 
P36L67: supply side of the system, air/lubricant separators, 
P36L68: aftercoolers, moisture separators, dryers and filters can 
P36L69: be the main items causing significant pressure drops. 
P36L70:  
P36L71: The maximum pressure drop from the supply side to 
P36L72: the points-of-use will occur when the compressed air 
P36L73: flow rate is highest. System components should be 
P36L74: selected based upon these conditions and the 
P36L75: manufacturer of each component should be requested 
P36L76: to supply pressure drop information under these 
P36L77: conditions. Filter elements should be changed when the 
P36L78: pressure drop approaches the maximum recommended 
P36L79: by the manufacturer. In many cases the element change 
P36L80: is dictated by energy losses. 
P36L81:  
P36L82: The distribution piping system is often diagnosed as 
P36L83: having excess pressure drop because a point-of-use 
P36L84: component cannot sustain the required down- stream 
P36L85: pressure. If a regulator is set at 85 psig and an upstream
