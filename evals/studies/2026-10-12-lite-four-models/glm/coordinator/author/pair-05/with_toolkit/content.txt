# Decision Note: April Washing Trial

**Recommendation:** Do not roll out yet. The trial shows a clear water-intensity improvement, but the missing soil-mix and quality data mean the approval condition cannot be verified.

**Numbers, with units reconciled.** March used 480,000 litres (480 m³) to wash 6,000 crates — 80 litres per crate. April used 420 m³ (420,000 litres) for 7,000 crates — 60 litres per crate. Both months cover the same line, count only fresh-water intake, exclude recycled water, and had no meter outages (S1). Water intensity fell by 20 litres per crate, a 25% reduction, while total fresh-water use fell 60 m³ (12.5%) even as volume rose 1,000 crates.

**What can be inferred.** Intensity — the right denominator, since crate volume changed — improved materially. The revised nozzle introduced 1 April (S2) is a plausible contributor.

**What cannot.** March ran a mix of lightly and heavily soiled crates; April's proportions were not logged, and staffing and hours changed (S2). If April's crates were lighter on average, part of the 25% gain may reflect workload, not the nozzle. The ledger contains no cleaning-quality or contamination measurements, and these are monthly totals, not controlled observations.

**Next step.** A rollout can be recommended only if water intensity improves without quality loss. Run a defined trial logging soil mix, staffing, and quality scores per batch; the missing evidence is exactly that paired measurement.

**Sources:** S1 (operations ledger, 30 April 2026); S2 (trial memo, 30 April 2026).
