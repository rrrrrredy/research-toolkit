# Decision Note: April Washing Trial — Water Use Assessment

**Prepared for:** Operations Manager, Cedar Packing | **Date:** 30 April 2026

## Did the trial reduce water use?

Yes, on both raw and normalized measures. March fresh-water intake was 480,000 litres for 6,000 washed crates; April was 420 cubic metres, which equals 420,000 litres, for 7,000 crates (S1). Absolute use fell 60,000 litres (12.5%) even though crate volume rose 16.7%.

## Reconciling units and denominators

The ledgers use different units — litres (March) versus cubic metres (April) — but the conversion is exact: 1 cubic metre = 1,000 litres (S1). Both meters cover the same washing line, exclude recycled water, and measure fresh-water intake only, with no outages, so the figures are directly comparable (S1). Because crate counts differ, the fair denominator is litres per crate: March 80 L/crate, April 60 L/crate — a 25% reduction in water intensity.

## What we can and cannot infer

The revised nozzle introduced 1 April is associated with the improvement (S2), but causation cannot be claimed: the data are total monthly counts, not randomized observations (S1), April's soil-mix proportions were not logged, and staffing and hours changed (S2). We also cannot confirm cleaning quality, since the ledger contains no quality or contamination measurements (S2).

## Recommended next step

Management will approve rollout only if water intensity improves *without* reducing cleaning quality (S2). Intensity has improved; quality evidence is the missing piece. **Conditional next step:** run a follow-up trial period with logged soil-mix, staffing and quality/contamination measurements before any rollout decision.

**Sources:** S1, S2.
