# Memo: Reminder pilot — on-time returns

**Descriptive results (S1).** During the pilot, 80% of reminder enrollees returned on time versus 60% of decliners — a 20-point gap. But the same 20-point gap existed before the pilot (75% vs 55%), since enrollment was voluntary (S2). Each group improved by only 5 points, and both were exposed to a fine-amnesty campaign during the pilot (S2).

**Causal assessment.** The 20-point gap is descriptive only; it reflects pre-existing differences between people who opted in and those who declined, not the reminders' effect. Even the 5-point improvement in each group cannot be attributed to reminders, because the amnesty affected both groups equally. With voluntary enrollment, no prior-trend data, and no recorded opt-in reasons (S2), the pilot design cannot support a causal claim. The most that can be said is that reminders did not widen the existing gap.

**Proposed next test (S2).** Next month, randomly offer reminders and record assignment, uptake, and return status. Before viewing results, management must set a minimum worthwhile benefit — I propose a 10-point difference in on-time rates between offered and not-offered groups, since the pilot suggests plausible confounding effects of this size.

**Decision criterion.** Adopt reminders only if the randomized comparison shows at least the preset difference; otherwise decline.
