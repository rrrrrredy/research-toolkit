# Cedar Packing: April Washing Trial Decision Note

## Verdict: Promising but Inconclusive

The April trial reduced water use, but the evidence does not yet support a full rollout.

## What the Numbers Show

After reconciling units (1 m³ = 1,000 L):

- **March**: 480,000 L ÷ 6,000 crates = **80 L/crate**
- **April**: 420,000 L ÷ 7,000 crates = **60 L/crate**

Water intensity fell **25%** from March to April, while throughput rose from 6,000 to 7,000 crates (S1). Total fresh-water intake dropped from 480,000 L to 420,000 L despite the higher volume.

## What Cannot Be Inferred

A rollout decision requires more than an intensity drop. S2 states that April's soil-load mix was not logged, and staffing and hours changed, so the 25% improvement cannot be attributed solely to the nozzle. S2 also notes the ledger contains no cleaning-quality or contamination measurements. Management's own approval bar—improved intensity **without** reducing cleaning quality—therefore remains unmet.

## Conditional Next Step

Approve an extended trial on the same washing line for May and June, with (1) logged soil-load proportions, (2) fixed staffing/hours or documented variance, and (3) cleaning-quality or swab-test measurements. If intensity holds at or below 65 L/crate with quality confirmed, proceed to rollout; otherwise, isolate variables before further investment.

## Missing Evidence

- Cleaning-quality or contamination data (S2)
- Logged soil-load mix for April (S2)
- Staffing and hours variance between months (S2)

**Sources**: S1 (operations ledger, 30 Apr 2026), S2 (trial memo, 30 Apr 2026)
