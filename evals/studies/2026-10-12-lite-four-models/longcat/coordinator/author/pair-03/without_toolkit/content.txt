# Decision Note: April Washing Trial Assessment

**To:** Operations Manager, Cedar Packing
**Date:** 30 April 2026
**Subject:** Evaluation of Revised Nozzle Trial — Fresh-Water Use in April

## Water-Use Findings

The April washing trial did reduce fresh-water use on the washing line. In March, 6,000 washed crates consumed 480,000 litres of fresh water. In April, 7,000 washed crates consumed 420 cubic metres of fresh water (equivalent to 420,000 litres) [S1]. Both meters cover the same washing line, exclude recycled water, and measure fresh-water intake only; there were no meter outages [S1].

On a per-crate basis, water intensity fell from **80 litres per crate** (480,000 ÷ 6,000) to **60 litres per crate** (420,000 ÷ 7,000), a reduction of 20 litres per crate (25%).

## Reconciliation of Units and Denominators

S1 reports March consumption in litres and April in cubic metres. One cubic metre equals 1,000 litres [S1], so April's 420 cubic metres converts to 420,000 litres. The denominator (washed crates) is consistent across both months, though monthly totals are not randomized observations [S1].

## What Can and Cannot Be Inferred

The per-crate water intensity improved. However, management cannot attribute this improvement solely to the revised nozzle. April handled a different (and unlogged) mix of soiled crates, and staffing and hours also changed [S2]. The ledger contains no cleaning-quality or contamination measurements [S2], so it is unknown whether cleaning quality was maintained.

## Conditional Next Step

Management will approve a rollout only if water intensity improves without reducing cleaning quality [S2]. The missing evidence is a controlled trial that logs crate-soiling levels, staffing, hours, and cleaning-quality metrics across comparable periods so the nozzle's effect can be isolated.
