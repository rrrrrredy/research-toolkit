# Vale Library Reminder Pilot: Assessment and Next Test

## Descriptive Results

The pilot's raw numbers show a 5-percentage-point improvement in both groups: the reminder group rose from 75% to 80% on-time returns, while the comparison group rose from 55% to 60% (S1). The *difference-in-differences* is zero: both groups gained equally.

## Causal Assessment

These results **do not demonstrate a causal improvement from reminders**. Three design flaws undermine causal inference:

| Threat | Evidence |
|--------|----------|
| **Self-selection** | Enrollment was voluntary, not randomized (S2). Borrowers opting in may differ systematically in conscientiousness or schedule predictability. |
| **Confounding event** | A fine-amnesty campaign reached both groups during the pilot (S2), plausibly explaining the parallel 5-point rise. |
| **Missing baseline trends** | Earlier trends and opt-in reasons were unrecorded (S2), preventing assessment of whether the groups were on diverging trajectories. |

The parallel improvement is consistent with the amnesty campaign as the true cause, with reminders adding no marginal effect.

## Proposed Next Test

Staff should **randomize the *offer* of reminders** next month, recording assignment, actual uptake, and return status (S2). This creates an **intent-to-treat** comparison that isolates the causal effect of the offer, even with imperfect compliance.

## Decision Criterion

Management must preset the **smallest worthwhile benefit** before seeing results (S2). A reasonable starting point: a **5 percentage-point absolute improvement in on-time returns** in the offered-versus-not-offered groups. If the lower bound of a 95% confidence interval exceeds this threshold, expand reminders; if it falls below, discontinue or redesign.
