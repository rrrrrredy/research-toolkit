## Cedar Packing: April Washing Trial Decision Note

**To:** Operations Manager, Cedar Packing  
**Date:** 30 April 2026  
**Subject:** April Washing Trial – Water Use Assessment

### Finding: Water Intensity Improved

The April trial reduced water intensity despite higher throughput. March used 480,000 litres for 6,000 crates (80 L/crate); April used 420,000 litres for 7,000 crates (60 L/crate) (S1). This represents a **25% reduction in water intensity** and **12.5% lower absolute consumption** with 16.7% more crates washed.

### Unit Reconciliation

S1 reports March in litres and April in cubic metres. Converting: 420 cubic metres × 1,000 = 420,000 litres. Both periods used the same meter scope (fresh-water intake only, excluding recycled water), permitting valid comparison (S1).

### What Can Be Inferred

The revised nozzle introduced 1 April (S2) coincides with the intensity improvement. The same meter, no outages, and consistent crate counting support measurement reliability (S1).

### What Cannot Be Inferred

- **Causation:** April's soil mix was unlogged; if heavily soiled crates dropped, reduced intensity may reflect easier cleaning, not nozzle performance (S2).
- **Quality trade-off:** No cleaning-quality or contamination data exist; the intensity gain may accompany worse outcomes (S2).
- **Scalability:** Staffing and hours changed—uncontrolled variables affecting replicability (S2).

### Conditional Next Step

**Proceed to controlled pilot** if: (a) quality metrics are added to the ledger, and (b) soil proportions are logged. Management's rollout condition—water intensity improvement without quality reduction—cannot be validated with current data (S2).

### Missing Evidence

- Cleaning-quality and contamination measurements
- Soil-mix proportions by period
- Staffing/hours impact isolation
