# Trial Cedar under the revised quote and export requirement

Cedar is now the eligible trial choice for the six-person support team. The revised quote is $5 per active user per month, so six seats cost $30, leaving $5 within the unchanged $35 subscription ceiling. Cedar includes scheduled weekly CSV exports, which the team now requires. Maple remains $28 for up to six users, but its manual exports no longer satisfy the changed requirement. The $2 subscription difference cannot compensate for a missing required capability. These are fictional plan terms and a constructed follow-up for this exercise, not verified market offers. ## What changed and what still holds

The original Maple recommendation matched the earlier requirements: manual weekly exports were acceptable, while Cedar's six seats cost $36 and exceeded the ceiling. Two inputs have changed together: Cedar's price is lower, and scheduled export is now mandatory. This reverses the eligible choice without implying that the earlier arithmetic or recommendation was wrong. The comparison uses the revised price only from this scenario's version 2; it does not rewrite the original quote.

Cedar's original export and single sign-on capabilities remain in the revised offer. Single sign-on is still optional, so it is not the deciding factor. Both plans retain monthly billing without a minimum term. Neither supplies measurements of reliability, support response, onboarding time or migration cost. The new quote therefore changes subscription eligibility, not evidence about service quality or total operating cost. No staff-time saving has been measured.

The unresolved CSV question also remains: neither source lists exported columns, and the required field list is still unavailable. Scheduled delivery of an unsuitable file would not solve the team's problem. The evidence supports choosing Cedar to trial, not concluding that it is ready for adoption or authorizing a purchase.

## Check the complete weekly export path

The support lead should obtain the required field list and confirm the revised six-seat quote before any authorized trial. A designated support operator should then configure a scheduled weekly export with representative test tickets, check whether the expected file arrives, and reconcile its tickets and required fields against the test set. Log missing fields, missing tickets and failed delivery rather than assuming that scheduling implies reliability. These are proposed checks, not reported test results.

Assign an owner and backup to inspect the export outcome and investigate failures. Adopt only after the required data and scheduled path have been checked and current terms remain within budget. If Cedar fails those checks, keep adoption pending and seek a compliant alternative. Maple becomes eligible again only if the scheduled-export requirement is explicitly relaxed or a supported scheduled feature is offered and verified; its lower price alone is insufficient.
